# Changelog

## Unreleased (v1.0.0, hard break)

### Known issues (v1)

- **A mode with fewer than about `5 * ndim` live points is not sampled reliably, and its weight keeps a bias.** A covariance-adapted walk needs that many points to learn a mode's shape: on one Gaussian with the shape of sepW_d18's minor mode, nlive 30, 60 and 120 (1.7, 3.3 and 6.7 `ndim`) give logZ 4.35 +- 0.07, 0.21 +- 0.05 and 0.03 +- 0.04 too high (400 seeds each, v1 walk). A 6% mode holds 30 live points through its posterior bulk at nlive 500 and 120 at nlive 2000, so sepW_d18 at nlive 500 (1.7 `ndim`) is 2.4 +- 1.2% light (400 seeds; it was 12.1 +- 1.7% light) with a logit sd of 0.31 to 0.48, and the d = 32 cells at nlive 500 (0.9 `ndim`) fail: sepW_d32 has a logit bias of -0.90 +- 0.30 and loses the mode in 25% of seeds (`N`: 23%), connW_d32 has a logit sd of 2.0. At nlive 2000 (3.75 `ndim`) sepW_d32 and connW_d32 are unbiased (+0.005 +- 0.005 and -0.043 +- 0.028) but sepM_d32, whose minor mode holds 4% of the points when it separates, still loses it in 10% of seeds (`N`: 20%; logit sd 1.26). The perfect sampler has no such bias (exact constrained draws: +0.1 +- 0.1% at sepW_d18, nlive 500, 2240 runs), so it is the sampler's limit, not the estimator's. `modes()` flags a mode below `5 * ndim` live points as unresolved; raise `nlive`.
- **The mode moves cost 6% of the run time at 1 ms per likelihood call.** Against the v1 no-hop arm from before the folds (global walk, no clustering), the time in the compiled chunks per chain step on an H100 (40 batched seeds, x64) is 6% higher at 1 ms per call (4% at nlive 500, 8% at 2000, at most 18%, at sepM_d32 with nlive 2000; 30 cells). It was 19% (13%, 26%, 54%) by the bake-off's measure, which also counts the first live points and the host-side results; by that measure it is now -2% (-5%, 0%, at most 17%), because the results of a batch are built faster than before the folds. What is left is the clustering (three per recluster, every quarter e-fold) and, while a run holds several clusters, one whitening of the proposal per chain step; a batch of runs pays for its busiest lane. Likelihood calls are unchanged (1.000x). The kernel compiles in 9.5 to 11.4 s on the H100 (was 11 to 21 s; 2.7 s before the mode tracking).

### Changes

- **`bench/validate.py` is the release gate; `validation/` is deleted (v1 PR 6).** One command runs tinyns on Gaussians (d = 2, 10, 32), Rosenbrock (d = 4, 10, at `walks = 25 * ndim`), the funnel, LogGamma, the eggbox and the two-mode mixtures `sepW_d10` and `sepW_d18` (at an `nlive` that gives the minor mode more than `5 * ndim` live points), with the seeds of a case as one batch of keys, and prints a PASS/FAIL table against the exact evidence and mode masses of `bench/targets.py`; the exit code is 1 on a failure.
  - *Criteria.* logZ bias under 3 se; scatter over `logzerr` in [0.75, 1.3] (a case fails only when the ratio is outside the band by more than 3 of its own standard errors, `1 / sqrt(2 (N - 1))`); for the mixtures the logit bias of the minor mode weight under 3 se, its sd at most 0.4 and no lost mode; every run converged and no mode flagged `unresolved`.
  - *Tiers.* `quick` (16 seeds, d <= 10, nlive 500, the mixture at 2000) takes about 6 minutes on 4 CPU cores; `full` (64 seeds, every case, the default nlive 1000, the mixtures at 2000 and 4000) is for a GPU or a Slurm node. `--json` keeps the per-seed values.
  - *Removed.* `validation/` (`run_validation.py`, `summarize_validation.py`, `compare_validation.py`, its 2-D `targets.py`) and its four test files; `RELEASE_CHECKLIST.md` is rewritten around the gate.
- **`bench/summarize.py` writes the head-to-head report.** It merges any number of `tinyns-bench-1` JSONL files (the GPU and the CPU sweep) into one Markdown report, with the same rows as CSV (`--csv`) and JSON (`--json`).
  - *Cells.* Per sampler and target: dlogZ +- se, the rms, scatter over `logzerr`, the fraction of runs within 3 `logzerr` of the truth, the mode-weight logit bias +- se and sd with a bootstrap CI, the lost fraction, the median calls, the median wall time (timeouts included, marked `>`), the hardware label, the live points the sampler really used, and timeouts and errors. Runs with `--opt` settings are their own sampler (`name [key=value]`), so a matched-settings rerun sits next to the default.
  - *Headline.* Per target family, the rank of the reference sampler (`--reference`, default `tinyns_v1`) and each competitor's calls, wall time, rms and logit sd relative to it, with the costs also at equal rms (the cost ratio times the squared rms ratio; the rank uses these), as geometric means over the targets where both are accurate (at least 90% of the runs finished; logZ bias within 3 se or 0.1 nats; for a mixture at most 10% of seeds lost a mode and the logit bias within 3 se or 0.1). Without records of the reference the report says so and prints the rest.
  - *Nautilus adapter.* `--opt pool=N` passes `pool=(None, N)`, so that Nautilus trains its networks in parallel on the cores it is given.
- **The mode moves cost less per step (same chains, same clusterings).** No change of the algorithm: the kernels, the cross-fitting and the clustering rules are those of the entry below.
  - *Chain steps.* A chain now carries the whitened offsets of its point to its fold's frames and whitens one point per step, the proposal, against the frames of all three folds in one product; before, every chain gathered its own copy of the 8 frame factors (`8 d^2` numbers per chain and step, 0.5 GB per step for 40 seeds at d = 32, nlive 2000), and a hop step whitened four points. The frames are factorized at the recluster and kept in the state (`State.mode_chol`, `mode_ichol`, `mode_logdet`, `mode_active`, `mode_eligible` replace `mode_scat`; checkpoints are `tinyns-ckpt-5`), not refactorized every step. A hop proposal does not depend on the chain's point, so a chain draws those of all its hop steps before it runs (`modes.hop_proposal`: one normal vector and three uniforms, the frame by inversion; `modes.propose_hop` is gone), and the chain is one `lax.scan` instead of three nested ones. A step picks the cheapest of three chain kernels its frames allow, through an unbatched `lax.switch` (`core._kernel_level`): plain symmetric walks while no clustering has two walk clusters or two eligible ones (every step of a unimodal run), a kernel that looks up only the two frames in use when no clustering has more, and one for all 8. The three make the same draws from the same keys and return the same chain (tested to 1e-9 in float64), so which one runs does not change the kernel's law; a batch of runs uses the level its busiest lane needs.
  - *Clustering.* The split search of all lanes (the three clusterings of every run of a batch) is one loop that searches one cluster per lane and round, so it takes as many rounds as the lane with the most clusters needs (plus two per split); before, it ran once per slot id used by any lane and again, for every lane, inside each lane's split loop (133 ms per recluster at d = 32 when one of 120 lanes split). The labels are identical to v1's on ten live sets saved from runs (sepW at d = 10 and 18 and gauss_d18, nlive 500 and 2000, before and after the modes separate; 24 clusterings each, started from the run's labels, from one cluster and from permuted slots) in float64, and in 29 of those 30 cases in float32 (one clustering of one cold start differs, by the batch-shape roundoff that already separates batched from single runs). The hard-EM assignment and the 2-means step are matrix products instead of triangular solves and distance sums, and the pair merge is skipped while no clustering has two clusters.
  - *Compilation.* XLA fused the normal generator into every consumer of its draws and drew the vector again in each. With the norm of the hop's direction that fusion compiled slowly on a GPU: 9 s of the 20 s of v1's d = 32, nlive 2000 cells, and 40 to 90 s at d = 32 and 64 once the hop proposals were drawn ahead. An `optimization_barrier` after the draws ends both.
  - *Host side.* `finalise` transforms the samples in one compiled kernel (blocks of 65536 rows) and slices lanes and labels on the host; a batch of 40 runs spent 3.7 to 8 s there (0.3 to 6 s now), per-lane compiles of small programs for the most part. `result.metadata["sampling_s"]` is the time in the compiled chunks (compilation, the first live points and the results excluded). Seeds drawn with replacement (a fold with too few points above `L*`) use one uniform each instead of `nlive` Gumbels.
  - *Cost* (H100, 40 seeds, x64, ms per chain step of the batch; `N` of v1 before the folds / v1 / now, compiled chunks only): sepW_d10 0.027 / 0.095 / 0.071 at nlive 500 and 0.044 / 0.201 / 0.122 at 2000; sepW_d18 0.023 / 0.158 / 0.073 and 0.040 / 0.287 / 0.112; sepM_d32 at nlive 2000 0.042 / 0.622 / 0.239; gauss_d18 at nlive 2000 0.036 / 0.250 / 0.077. A recluster of the 120 clusterings takes 4 to 8 ms at nlive 500 and 8 to 32 ms at 2000 (v1: 5 to 42 and 10 to 214). Compile: 9.5 to 11.4 s, median 10.2 (v1: 11.2 to 20.9, median 13.6). On a CPU one float32 run of gauss_d18 at nlive 500 takes 3.1 s (v1: 12.4 s) after a 4 s compile, sepW_d18 at nlive 1000 9.8 s (44.8 s).
  - *Re-validation* (H100, the 60 cells x 40 seeds of the entry below, sha 87d92b3; tables in `/hildafs/projects/phy220048p/magana/darksirens-core-data/tinyns_h100_2026-09-30/v1_opt/`). The random streams differ (the hop's draws), so the comparison is statistical: against the PR 154 run, the 24 `B_ell` logit biases differ by z = -0.02 on average (rms 0.87, at most 1.6), the 60 logZ biases by -0.13 (rms 0.39, at most 1.1), and the logit sd is 0.94x (geometric mean; 0.50 to 1.35). Most cells without a hop are the same chains: 13 of the 16 control cells and 5 of the 8 `N` cells at nlive 2000 with d <= 18 reproduce PR 154 to the three digits printed (the others diverge after a roundoff-level flip of one decision). At nlive 2000 every resolvable cell with d <= 18 has a logit bias within 1.5 se of 0 (|bias| <= 0.007) and a logit sd of 0.021 to 0.039; sepW_d32 is at -0.001 +- 0.005 and connW_d32 at -0.007 +- 0.021. The cells below `5 * ndim` keep their bias or scatter (sepW_d18 at nlive 500: -0.09 +- 0.06, sd 0.36; sepW_d32 at 500: -0.77 +- 0.28; sepM_d32 at 2000: sd 0.63, 17% of seeds lose the mode). Lost modes over the unresolvable cells: 85 of 240 seed-modes (PR 154: 73; the unchanged `N` arm moved from 89 to 79). The one resolvable mode more than 2 se from 0, mix3_d10 mode 1 at nlive 500 (-0.019 +- 0.007), is a fluctuation: 400 seeds on CPUs give +0.002 +- 0.002 and +0.005 +- 0.003 for its two minor modes (v1: -0.002 +- 0.002 and -0.003 +- 0.003). Controls (`B_ell`): logZ biases within 2.0 se, scatter/logzerr 0.77 to 1.23. Likelihood calls 1.000x `N` (at most 1.004).
  - *Tests.* The 10^6-chain invariance gate: z = +1.0 with the local walk and +0.8 without in float64 (+0.2 and -0.2 in float32); frames that hold the seed are 21 to 23 sigma low. New: a chain that reads its fold from the stacked frames, or the compact copy, returns the chain of that fold's frames alone; the three kernels agree on a run's states; the hop proposal picks frames by volume and fills their ellipsoids uniformly.
- **`modes()` flags a mode unresolved below `5 * ndim` live points (was 3).** `UNRESOLVED_PER_DIM = 5`: the population a mode needs for its own walk to mix (see the known issues).
- **`modes()` no longer loses a mode to a few late points.** A label with at most `ndim` distinct live points joins the nearest other label; the distance is now to that label's live points at its own posterior median, not at the small label's, when the other may be down to two or three points that span no covariance. On the 3-D two-mode test target (nlive 400) the clustering sometimes cuts three points off the minor mode in its last steps; they were attached to the main mode, the gap test then merged the two modes, and 4 of 216 runs reported one mode (v1: 1 of 216). All 216 now report two.

- **Cross-fitted mode moves and a local random walk (the fix of the two v1 known issues).** The live slots form three folds (slot index mod 3; private `Config._folds`). Each new point is seeded from a live point of the fold of the dead slot it fills, and its chain's kernel is built from the points outside that fold only: the hop's ellipsoids and the walk's covariances come from a clustering of those points (three clusterings, one per fold, as lanes of one call). Neither the seed, nor its influence on the clustering, nor its ancestors enter its kernel, so for independent live points the chain's end point is exactly uniform in the constrained region; this is emcee's two-ensemble split with three ensembles. It replaces the leave-one-out downdate (`modes.downdate`, `modes.chain_frames` and `State.fitted` are gone; `State.label` is `(3, nlive)` and the cluster statistics have a leading axis of 3; checkpoints are `tinyns-ckpt-4`). The walk proposes `y = x + s L_c(x) z` with `c(x)` the nearest cluster frame and accepts with the Hastings ratio `N(x | y, s^2 S_c(y)) / N(y | x, s^2 S_c(x))` (private `Config._local=False`: the live covariance everywhere); with fewer than two clusters of 3 points it uses the live covariance of the points outside the fold. `result.labels` holds a point's slot in the clustering of the next fold, as `j * 8 + slot`, and `modes()` merges the labels that cover one mode. A split now moves the part without the cluster's first row to the new slot, so batched and single runs agree whatever sign their eigensolvers give an eigenvector.
  - *Diagnosis of the 1-2% underweighting.* The suspected cause, the seed's influence on the other points' labels, is nil: re-clustering a fixed two-mode live set with and without the seed changed no other label in 4.4e5 chains (sepW and connW at d = 10 and 18, nlive 500 and 2000), so the downdate was exact there. The cause was mixing inside the minor mode: its shape differs from the main mode's (variances 0.03 to 30 in the main mode's frame), and the walk in the covariance of all live points accepted 0.08 of its steps there at d = 18 against 0.26 in the main mode (0.06 against 0.26 at d = 32). Chains returned near-copies of their seeds, which sat inside frames fitted to their parents and hopped out too readily. On sepW_d18 at nlive 2000 the minor mass was 1.25 +- 0.16% low (408 seeds); the local walk alone (with the downdate) gives +0.22 +- 0.16%, two folds 0.01 +- 0.16% and this version +0.14 +- 0.15% (400 seeds each). Perfect nested sampling has no bias in this estimator (-0.1 to +0.2 +- 0.1%, 700 runs per target).
  - *Diagnosis of d = 32.* The targets are well posed (truncated mass below 3e-15), but the minor mode holds 0.9 `ndim` live points at nlive 500 and 3.75 at 2000 (see the known issues). Two more effects were the sampler's. The seed-free covariance of all live points still held each seed's ancestors and the other mode: on sepW_d32 at nlive 500 it left logZ 0.24 +- 0.05 high with or without the hop (120 seeds), and kernels fitted outside the seed's fold leave 0.04 +- 0.04 (4 folds; this version 0.10 +- 0.09 in 40 seeds). And without the hop the minor mode's population outlived its volume (twice too many points 30 e-folds after it separated), which is `N`'s Z2/truth of 2.7.
  - *Re-validation* (H100, 60 cells x 40 seeds, `N` and `B_ell`; tables in `/hildafs/projects/phy220048p/magana/darksirens-core-data/tinyns_h100_2026-09-30/v1_mmfix/compact.txt`). At nlive 2000 every resolvable cell with d <= 18 has a logit bias within 1.2 se of 0 (|bias| <= 0.007 +- 0.006; v1: -0.005 to -0.022) and a logit sd of 0.016 to 0.038, 0.8 to 1.24x the exact-draw floor; sepW_d32 is at +0.005 +- 0.005 (v1: -0.110 +- 0.009). At nlive 500 and d = 10 the biases are -0.026 to +0.014 (v1: -0.003 to -0.063). The cells that fail are the small-population ones listed in the known issues: logit sd above 0.4 at sepW_d18 (0.48), connW_d18 (0.42), sepW_d32 and connW_d32 at nlive 500 and at sepM_d32 at nlive 2000; one bias above 3 se (sepW_d32 at nlive 500). Over the 21 resolvable modes the logit sd is 0.142 of `N`'s (v1: 0.136). Controls: every logZ bias is within 2.1 se and scatter/logzerr is 0.77 to 1.20 (`N`: rosen_d4 at nlive 2000 1.33). Compilation takes 11 to 21 s. Higher-power runs of this version (200 to 400 seeds): sepW_d18 +0.14 +- 0.15%, connW_d18 +0.22 +- 0.24% and sepM_d18 -0.02 +- 0.20% at nlive 2000; sepW_d10 -0.20 +- 0.49% and connW_d10 -0.69 +- 0.72% at nlive 500.
  - *Tests.* The invariance gate now runs 10^6 chains (`pytest -m slow`; it resolves 0.5% of the minor fraction at 3 sigma): z = +0.7 with the local walk and +0.9 without, and frames fitted to a set that holds the seed are 21 sigma low. A fast test cuts one box into two frames of different shapes: the local walk keeps the uniform law and the same walk without the Hastings ratio is 17 sigma off.
- **The inter-mode hop is always on (v1 PR 4: the bake-off winner `B_ell`).** Every 10th chain step is an independence Metropolis-Hastings step from the uniform law on the union of the eligible clusters' moment-matched ellipsoids (`|L^-1 (x - mu)|^2 <= d + 2`, a frame chosen in proportion to its volume), so `q(x)` is proportional to the number of ellipsoids containing `x` and the hop is accepted iff the proposal is in the cube, above `L*` and `U < q(x) / q(x')`; with fewer than two eligible frames the step is a random-walk step. The v1 bake-off (5 arms, 152 cells x 40 seeds on an H100; report: `/hildafs/projects/phy220048p/magana/darksirens-core-data/tinyns_h100_2026-09-30/v1_bakeoff/REPORT.md`, per-cell tables in `compact.txt`) found that it cuts the seed-to-seed logit sd of minor-mode masses to 0.136 of the no-hop urn baseline `N` (geometric mean over all cells, about 7x), at 1.000x `N`'s likelihood calls, about 1.9x the exact-draw floor, with hop acceptance 0.80 / 0.69 / 0.61 / 0.39 at d = 4 / 10 / 18 / 32; e.g. sepW_d4 at nlive 500: logit sd 0.059 (`N` 0.378), sepW_d10: 0.101 (`N` 0.535). Its cost is about 9% of run time at 1 ms per likelihood call (6-9% at nlive 500, 11-14% at 2000) and 7-11 s of compilation. The affine swap `C` tied on precision but had more biased cells and lower acceptance; the Student-t mixture `B_t` collapsed with `d` (acceptance 0.02 at d = 32) and the alternation `BC` did not beat its parts: all three are deleted, with their tests. `Config._mode` is replaced by the private `Config._hop` (default `True`; `False` replaces the hop by random-walk steps and still clusters, for comparisons). The clustering now runs in every run, so `State`'s and `Dead`'s mode fields are always present (never `None`) and `metadata["mode"]` is gone. `bench/bakeoff/` keeps the arms `N` (`_hop=False`) and `B_ell` for re-tests (62 cells) and records `result.modes()` per seed.
- **`modes()` reads the cluster labels of the run; `tinyns/clusters.py` is deleted.** Each sample's cluster slot at its death (the final live points: at the end) is recorded in the new `result.labels` (saved in the result `.npz`; `None` for a result built by hand, which reports one mode), and `modes()` builds the modes from it instead of re-clustering a posterior draw with the host-side v0.x numpy tracker: labels with less than one effective posterior sample join the heaviest; labels with at most `ndim` distinct live points at their posterior median join the nearest label (as before); labels merge, one pair at a time, unless they leave a gap of `SEPARATION_SIGMA = 3` within-cluster deviations at each of their two posterior medians (was: at the earlier one); then every sample is relabelled by its nearest mode (posterior-weighted frames), so a mode keeps its points from before the run split it off. The per-mode report is unchanged (`mass`, `urn_sd`, `min_live`, `isolation_iteration`, `unresolved` below `3 ndim`, now `5 ndim`). It runs no clustering and compiles nothing (10-60 ms per call). On identical runs without the hop (`_hop=False`, the v1 PR 3 default), 5-D and 10-D bananas at nlive 500 and 1000 (x64 off and on, 48 seeds each) report a false second mode in 2 of 384 runs (the old `modes()`: 1 of 384), and the 25% mode of the 3-D test target at nlive 400 is found in 96 of 96 runs (old: 85); on runs with the hop, 5 of 384 (old `modes()` on the same runs: 4 of 384; every false mode is flagged unresolved, mass 0.01-0.05) and 96 of 96 (old: 78). A 6% mode at 4-D, nlive 500, is found in 96 of 96 runs either way, and the reported masses equal the oracle's to 1e-4. The compiled-kernel caches (`tinyns.callables`) hold 8 configs (was 32): a compiled run with the clustering holds about 2000 memory mappings on a CPU, and 32 configs per kernel reached Linux's default limit of 65530 and aborted the XLA compiler in a long test session.
- **On-device mode tracking (`tinyns/modes.py`; v1 PR 3).** The live points are clustered on the device every `max(1, round(nlive / (4 num_delete)))` steps (a quarter of an e-fold), through an unbatched `lax.cond` in the chunk loop: warm-started hard EM, merges, then top-down Fisher splits (2-means from four starts, refined by hard EM; `J >= max(25, 1.5 (d + 2))` after a small-sample deflation), in 8 fixed slots. PR 3 added the bake-off arms behind a private `Config._mode` (`"N"`, `"B_ell"`, `"B_t"`, `"C"`, `"BC"`; PR 4 kept `B_ell`), with every 10th chain step the arm's move (a schedule fixed in advance). Each chain refits the frames without its own seed (a rank-one downdate of its cluster's statistics, then the pooled covariance, every frame and the eligibility recomputed), so its kernel does not depend on its start: on a fixed two-mode live set, 10^5 chains from uniform seeds end in the minor mode at its volume fraction within 3 sigma for every arm, and without the downdate the fraction is 7 to 9 sigma low. `State` gains `label`, `fitted` and the cluster statistics (`mode_mu`, `mode_scat`, `mode_count`), all checkpointed; `Dead` gains `label`, `hops`, `hop_tries`, `nclusters` and `neligible`; `metadata` gains `hops`, `hop_tries`, `hop_acceptance` and `mode_history`, and `acceptance` is the random-walk acceptance (the step scale adapts to it). `bench/bakeoff/` runs, emits and summarizes the bake-off.
- **The proposal covariance leaves out the chains' seeds.** Each step builds the live-covariance Cholesky factor from the points above `L*` other than the step's seeds (`core._proposal_mask`); it keeps them only if fewer than `ndim + 1` points would remain. A chain's proposal no longer depends on its own start, so for independent live points its end point is exactly uniform inside the contour for any walk length. With the seed in the covariance, its leverage (order `ndim / nlive`) pulled the chain inward and overestimated logZ: on correlated Gaussians with 500 live points and `walks = 6 * ndim`, +0.17 +- 0.03 nats at 32-D (now -0.02 +- 0.04) and +1.13 +- 0.12 at 64-D (now +0.36 +- 0.08). The bias depended on `nlive` (32-D: +0.33, +0.17, +0.02 for 250, 500, 1000 live points). Numerics change for every run.
- **The default `walks` is `max(25, 6 * ndim, ndim**2 // 6)`** (`tinyns.core.default_walks`), was `max(25, 6 * ndim)`. It is unchanged up to 36 dimensions; above, it is `ndim / 6` steps per dimension (266 at 40-D, 384 at 48-D, 522 at 56-D, 682 at 64-D; 1.1x, 1.33x, 1.56x and 1.78x the old cost). With the seeds out of the covariance, what remains is mixing early in a run, while the prior box cuts the contours: the likelihood rank along a chain has an integrated autocorrelation time of 2 to 3 `ndim` steps there (about 9 steps later, at any `ndim`). At 56-D and 64-D, `6 * ndim` to `7 * ndim` left +0.24 to +0.36 nats. With the new default, 26 Gaussian cells from 2 to 64 dimensions with 250 to 2000 live points (20 to 80 seeds each; x64 on and off; `num_delete` 1 and 50) have |bias| < 2 sigma, except a 24-D cell at +2.06 sigma and a 2-D float32 gate cell at +2.2 sigma (-0.004 +- 0.008 over 200 more seeds). Scatter/logzerr is 0.66 to 1.24 per cell; over all 700 runs, (logz - truth) / logzerr has mean +0.05 +- 0.04 and sd 0.93. A 10-D Rosenbrock needs 12 to 25 `* ndim` (at the default its logZ scatters 2.3 times `logzerr`); the README says so.
- **A fresh full-JAX core (`tinyns/core.py`).** `Config(ndim, nlive=1000, num_delete=None, walks=None)`, the `State` and `Dead` pytrees, `init`, `step`, `finalise`, `delta_logz` and the driver `run`. Each step deletes the `num_delete` lowest live points (default `max(1, nlive // 10)`) and replaces them with fixed-length constrained random walks (no retry loop; an unmoved chain returns its seed), seeded without replacement above `L*`. The step-scale adaptation runs inside `step`. With `num_delete=1` the chain is not vmapped and out-of-cube proposals skip the likelihood; otherwise all lanes are evaluated and `metadata["ncall_valid"]` counts the in-cube ones.
- **Evidence with several deaths per step** (birth/death contours): live counts `m, ..., m-k+1` per step and `m, ..., 1` for the final live points; `finalise` recomputes the weights, `logz` and `logzerr = sqrt(sum_i dH_i / n_i)` in float64. The last sample takes the remaining prior volume, so a constant likelihood gives its value exactly. `result.nlive_i` and `result.num_delete` are new; the insertion test ranks new points among the `nlive - num_delete` survivors of their step. Result `.npz` files are `tinyns-result-npz-v3`.
- **Driver.** A jitted `lax.while_loop` runs chunks of up to four e-folds of steps into a preallocated dead buffer; `n_steps`, `dlogz`, `maxiter` and `maxcall` are traced, and the host syncs once per chunk (chunks grow to about 45 s). `maxcall` is new; a likelihood plateau ends the run with status `plateau`.
- **API.** `NestedSampler(loglike, prior_transform, ndim, nlive=1000, *, num_delete=None, walks=None).run(key, *, dlogz=0.1, maxiter=None, maxcall=None, progress=False, checkpoint=None, batched_data=False)`. Exports: `NestedSampler`, `Config`, `init`, `step`, `finalise`, `NestedSamplingResult`, `LogZBootstrap`.
- **Checkpoints (`tinyns-ckpt-3`, `tinyns/checkpoint.py`).** `run(key, checkpoint=path)` resumes from `path` if it exists and otherwise starts fresh; either way it writes `path` at a chunk boundary at most every 10 minutes (`core._CHECKPOINT_SECONDS`) and at the end. One `.npz`, written atomically (temporary file in the same directory, `fsync`, `os.replace`), holds the `State` leaves (the PRNG key as `uint32` key data plus the name of a typed key's implementation, so raw and typed keys round-trip), the starting key, the dead rows of each run, and `meta_json`: the resolved `Config`, the tinyns version, the x64 flag, the float dtype, the batch size, `batched_data`, the call counters (Python ints), `wall_time_s`, `compile_s` and the chunk count. A different config, x64 flag, dtype, key or batch is refused with a `ValueError` naming it; any other file format is refused (no readers for old formats). A checkpoint stopped by `converged`, `maxiter` or `maxcall` is re-tested against the new call's limits, so a run continues with a larger `maxiter`, and a finished checkpoint returns its result without new steps. There is no `resume()` method.
- **Determinism.** A resumed run is bit-identical to an uninterrupted one on CPU (tested with x64 on and off, for `num_delete` 1 and 5, raw and typed keys, after a crash mid-run and resumed with a different chunk schedule). The design: each step splits the key carried in `State`, so the random stream depends on the step count only; chunk lengths still follow the wall clock (about 45 s) and only decide where the host syncs. `metadata` gains `wall_time_s` (was `wall_time`; summed over resumes), `compile_s` (the chunk kernel's compile, timed by a zero-step chunk, summed over resumes) and `resumed`; `chunks` is summed over resumes.
- **Progress.** `progress=True` prints one line per chunk: `niter`, `logz`, the dlogz remainder, `ncall`, calls per second in the chunk, the chain acceptance in the chunk, the step scale and the status; for a batch, the running runs and the ranges.
- **Batched runs.** `run(keys)` with a leading batch axis (`jax.random.split(key, B)`, typed or raw) runs `B` independent runs in one compiled program and returns a list of `B` results. The `while_loop` is not vmapped: its body vmaps `step` over the runs, so the loop predicate (any run still running) and future `lax.cond` predicates stay unbatched; a finished run is frozen with `jnp.where` and its rows are a prefix of the chunk buffer. `maxcall` applies to each run. `batched_data=True` gives every array leaf of a pytree `loglike`/`prior_transform` a leading axis of `B` (one dataset per run; hoisted closure constants stay shared), so a mock or SBC campaign compiles once. Runs are not bit-identical to the separate runs of their keys (vmap changes XLA's fusion and summation order): in float64 they agree to roundoff; in float32 they can diverge late in a run and agree in distribution. On a CPU, B = 8 runs of a 5-D Gaussian (`nlive=1000`) take 2.3 s against 2.35 s for 8 sequential runs on one core, 0.71 s against 0.95 s on 8 cores; with `nlive=200`, 0.16 s against 0.29 s on 8 cores.
- **Removed:** `block_size`, `replacement_chains`, `cluster_swap`, `callback`, `checkpoint_path` and `resume` (replaced by `run(checkpoint=path)`), `tinyns/loop.py`, `tinyns/types.py`, `tinyns/checkpoint.py`, `benchmarks/`, `tools/ab_bitwise.py` and the `ab-bitwise` CI job. Numerics change on purpose: no result is bit-identical to v0.x.
- **`modes()` no longer splits a curved unimodal posterior.** Replacement chains get stuck in the narrow tips of a Rosenbrock banana, and the seed copies and short moves they return leave clumps of near-identical live points there, 7 to 50 deviations from the bulk along the discriminant. The split test cut these clumps off and the gap test kept them as modes, flagged unresolved: 26 of 384 runs of the 5-D and 10-D bananas at nlive 500 and 1000 (x64 off and on, 48 seeds each) reported two modes. A cluster with at most `ndim` distinct live points at its own posterior median is now dropped (its samples go to the nearest remaining cluster), since so few points span no volume, and the gap counts repeated points once. All 384 runs now report one mode and no warning. Detection is unchanged on two-mode targets: a 6% minor mode at 4-D, nlive 500, is found in 96 of 96 runs (minor mass 0.031-0.107), and the 25% mode of the 3-D test target at nlive 400 in 85 of 96, as before. The k-deletion `n(t)` and birth reconstruction were checked: the live count at every death equals `nlive_i`.

## v0.2.5 (2026-10-02)

- **New default on the fast path**: `cluster_swap=True`. Mode weights of separated modes no longer drift with the seed when each mode is large enough. Replacement chains start from live points and cannot cross between separated modes, so each mode's live count did a random walk. On a real 18-D posterior with a 16% minor mode, two seeds gave 7% and 32%. tinyns now clusters the live points on the host between blocks and tracks the clusters across blocks. Once two clusters each have a volume share that predicts at least 2 x `ndim` live points, a tenth of the chain steps become an affine swap `u' = mu_b + L_b L_a^-1 (u - mu_a)` into another cluster's frame. The swap is accepted when `u'` is in the cube, above the threshold and nearest to frame `b`, with the Metropolis factor `det L_b / det L_a`. This is exact for any frames. The frame of the chain's seed is refit without the seed, and the rwalk keeps the global live covariance.
- Measured with tinyns on two-mode Gaussian targets with a 6% minor mode (nlive 500, 20 seeds per cell, swap off -> on). Logit seed scatter of the minor-mode mass: 0.46 -> 0.15 and 0.36 -> 0.10 at 4-D (two minor-mode shapes), 0.83 -> 0.16 at 10-D, 1.28 -> 0.39 at 18-D (200 seeds; the prototype gives 0.36 there). Mean mass with the swap on: 0.060, 0.060, 0.057 +/- 0.002 and 0.058 +/- 0.001. An eligibility threshold of 3 x `ndim` switched the swap off too early at 18-D (0.52, 40 seeds, one seed lost the mode). logZ bias and scatter are unchanged, and likelihood calls fell by 2% to 5%. A minor mode with fewer live points than about 2 x `ndim` (20 at 10-D, 36 at 18-D) gets no reliable benefit: the swap stays off or flickers, and the weights drift as before.
- On a real 18-D posterior (darksirens rung 5, BPL+2G, low-H0 mode holding 18-22% of the mass by a split-prior reference) at nlive 1000, 8 seeds per arm: the swap gives a low-H0 mass of 0.188 on average (logit sd 0.30) against 0.135 (logit sd 0.44) without it, and logZ -678.45 +/- 0.23 against the reference -678.3 +/- 0.2. At nlive 500 both arms come out near 0.08: the mode holds only about 28 live points (1.5 x `ndim`) when it is first detected, too few for the swap.
- A unimodal run that the clustering does not split is bit-identical to v0.2.4 for the same seed; a test pins fixed-seed fingerprints. On synthetic single ellipsoids, uniform boxes and the 13-D Gaussian there were no false splits. Real posteriors do get split sometimes: 3 of 10 seeds on a 13-D mock spectral-siren fit and 2 of 2 on another, none on a 16-D dark-siren fit. The move is exact either way, and those runs agree with v0.2.4 and the reference within `logzerr` (13-D means -58.578 vs -58.581, reference -58.623 +/- 0.029). The clustering runs on the host and never touches the PRNG stream. It costs about 12 ms per update at 13-D (one update every 128 iterations): 2.0 s of a 29 s run on a 13-D Gaussian.
- `result.metadata` reports `cluster_count_history` (`[iteration, clusters]` at each change), `cluster_swap_proposals`, `cluster_swap_accepts`, `cluster_min_population` and `cluster_modes`. `cluster_modes` gives, for each mode, its mass, its smallest live count between detection and its posterior median, and `urn_logit_sd`, the seed scatter of `logit(mass)` the drift would cause without the swap. Also `cluster_host_s`.
- `cluster_swap` applies to `sample="rwalk"`, `kernel="jax"`, `bound="none"`, `rwalk_proposal="live-cov"`, `replacement_chains=1` and `jax_block_size > 1`. Elsewhere it defaults to False, and an explicit `cluster_swap=True` raises `NotImplementedError`. Checkpoints record it and the cluster tracker's state. A checkpoint without the key reads as `cluster_swap=False`, so resume a v0.2.4 checkpoint with `cluster_swap=False`.

## v0.2.4 (2026-10-02)

- **Changed default**: `min_accepts=0` (was 1). A replacement chain that never moved is now kept as a copy of its seed, instead of being discarded and retried. The retry rule was biased: chains fail to move more often where the proposal fits poorly, so retrying under-sampled those regions. Measured with tinyns itself on two-mode Gaussian targets with a true 6% minor mode (nlive 500): at 4-D the old rule gave 2.9% +/- 0.3% and a logZ bias of +0.13 +/- 0.03, the new default 6.3% +/- 0.4% and +0.02 +/- 0.03 (40 seeds each); at 10-D, 1.7% +/- 0.6% against 6.7% +/- 1.7% (18 seeds each). Call counts are unchanged. A kept chain must still end inside the constraint, and an unmoved chain only counts if its seed is strictly above the threshold. Pass `min_accepts=1` for the old behaviour; checkpoints written with the old default need `min_accepts=1` to resume.

## v0.2.3 (2026-10-01)

- A loop over mock datasets no longer recompiles for every dataset. The fast-path kernel caches (block kernel, rwalk kernel and the initial live-point pass) were keyed on the identity of `loglike` and `prior_transform`, so each `jax.tree_util.Partial(loglike_fn, data_i)` built and compiled a new block kernel. They are now keyed on the callable's structure: a pytree callable's treedef and static leaves, with its array leaves passed as jit arguments, so JAX's jit cache keys the compiled program on their shapes and dtypes. On CPU, four `Partial` datasets of the same shape (1-D, 10k points, fixed `maxiter`) took 2 backend compiles for every new dataset in v0.2.2 (block kernel and live-point pass) and none in v0.2.3. Each result is bit-identical to a fresh run on that dataset. The live-point pass is now cached too; v0.2.2 compiled it again on every run, even for the same callable.
- Finished runs no longer keep their datasets alive. The `lru_cache(maxsize=32)` held up to 32 callables and so their data: in a four-dataset probe, v0.2.2 retained all four. The caches now hold no array leaves. Closures and plain functions are cached by identity through weak references, and their hoisted constants are dropped when the closure is garbage collected, so a dataset is freed once you drop it. Each closure object still compiles its own kernels; use a pytree callable to share one compile across datasets.
- Plain functions and small closures give bit-identical results to v0.2.2: a test pins fixed-seed `logz`, `samples`, `samples_u`, `logl` and `ncall` for the block and per-iteration paths. `Partial` and hoisted-closure runs were also checked to be bit-identical.
- `result.metadata` reports wall-time telemetry: `wall_time_s` (sampling wall time of the call), `compile_s` (time to the end of the first block, which carries the compiles) and `mean_ms_per_call` (wall time per likelihood call after the first block). Progress lines show `calls/s` since the first block, and callback states carry `calls_per_s`. There are no new arguments.
- The README notes that separate processes in a campaign can share compiles through JAX's persistent cache (`jax.config.update("jax_compilation_cache_dir", path)`). On CPU, a second process with a different dataset read its compiled programs from the cache and reached the end of its first block in 0.72 s instead of 1.62 s.

## v0.2.2 (2026-10-01)

- With the default single chain (`replacement_chains=1`), a live-cov proposal that leaves the unit cube no longer calls the likelihood. It is rejected either way, so it now takes a `lax.cond` branch that returns `-inf` without evaluating. `ncall` counts likelihood evaluations actually made; the acceptance rate and the batch bookkeeping use the proposal count. Samples and logZ are identical to v0.2.1; `logl` differs only at the 1e-13 rounding level, from XLA fusing the call inside the branch. Measured on an H100 against v0.2.1: a 13-D mock spectral-siren fit made 55% fewer calls (521k to 233k) and ran 1.8x faster (226 s to 124 s); a 1-D dark-siren fit made 12% fewer calls and ran 1.12x faster. With more than one chain, a `vmap`ped `cond` would evaluate both branches, so nothing is skipped.

## v0.2.1 (2026-09-30)

- On the fast path, closures now get the pytree-callable treatment automatically. A `loglike` or `prior_transform` without array leaves is traced once (`jax.make_jaxpr`), and every jaxpr constant with at least 4096 elements (a captured `jax.Array` or `np.ndarray`, including operands of a nested `jax.jit` call) is passed to the compiled kernels as a jit argument; smaller constants stay embedded. On a 1-D GW likelihood with 2.5M-row arrays (H100), the closure form took two backend compiles of 74 s and 78 s (first block after 172 s), and the pytree form 5.3 s and 6.8 s (first block after 23 s). With hoisting, the same closure reaches its first block after 21.6 s, the same as the pytree form (21.3 s). Through the darksirens ladder harness (a plain closure), a 1-D dark-siren run on the mock R1 fixture dropped from 861 s and 12.0 GB peak RSS to 387 s and 2.2 GB, against dynesty's 679 s. A 4-D real-data run dropped from 4.8 GB to 1.5 GB peak RSS. There is no API change. Closures with only small constants and plain functions are not traced into a new form and give bit-identical results to v0.2.0. A hoisted closure gives the same results as the equivalent `jax.tree_util.Partial`. If tracing fails, the callable keeps closure semantics and one debug-level log message is emitted per callable. Arrays captured inside the body of an inner `jax.jit` function stay embedded. Pytree callables remain the explicit form.
- `walks` now defaults to 12 in 1-D. In a 20-seed sweep, 1-D was unbiased from 10 walks (bias 0.2 x `logzerr`); from 2-D on, the default stays `max(25, 6 * ndim)`.

## v0.2.0 (2026-09-30)

- Added `rwalk_proposal="live-cov"`: each rwalk step is `step_scale * L @ z`, with `L` the Cholesky factor of the live-point covariance in the unit cube. Moves that leave the cube are rejected rather than reflected, and the step scale always adapts toward `rwalk_target_accept`. It is supported for unbounded `sample="rwalk"`, `kernel="jax"` with a fixed `replacement_chains`; other combinations raise `NotImplementedError`.
- **Changed defaults**: `NestedSampler(loglike, prior_transform, ndim).run(key)` and `run_static_nested` now run the fast path: `sample="rwalk"`, `kernel="jax"`, `rwalk_proposal="live-cov"`, `jax_block_size=32`, `walks=max(25, 6 * ndim)` and an initial `step_scale=0.5`. The old defaults were `sample="prior"`, `kernel="python"`, `rwalk_proposal="isotropic"`, `jax_block_size=1`, `walks=25` and `step_scale=0.1`; pass them explicitly to keep the old behavior. The default `kernel="jax"` needs JAX-traceable `loglike` and `prior_transform`.
- Where live-cov is unsupported (`kernel="python"`, a bound, or a `replacement_chain_schedule`), an unset `rwalk_proposal` falls back to `"isotropic"` with `step_scale=0.1` and `jax_block_size=1`, and `sample="prior"` defaults to `kernel="python"`. An explicit `rwalk_proposal="live-cov"` in those combinations still raises.
- Why: the fixed isotropic unit-cube step (`step_scale=0.1`) did not follow the contracting live set, so acceptance collapsed and the cost per iteration grew geometrically at d >= 4. On anisotropic correlated Gaussians, d = 2..18, live-cov keeps the likelihood calls per iteration flat at `walks`. Unbiased evidence needs `walks` of about 5-6 x `ndim`: `walks=25` biased logZ high by +0.5 nats at 13D and +1.4 nats at 18D, while `walks >= 5 * ndim` was within about 0.2 nats, with seed scatter matching the reported `logzerr`.
- With `kernel="jax"`, the initial live points are now evaluated in one compiled pass.
- `loglike` and `prior_transform` may be JAX pytree callables (`jax.tree_util.Partial(loglike_fn, data)`, an equinox-style Module, a registered dataclass with `__call__`). On the fast path (unbounded JAX rwalk: block kernel, per-iteration kernel, rescue draws and the initial live-point pass) their array leaves are passed to the compiled kernels as jit arguments instead of being embedded as constants. On a GW likelihood with 2.5M-row selection arrays (H100) this cut compile time from 76 s to 15 s and peak host RSS from 10.7 GB to 2.8 GB. Detection is automatic; plain functions and closures give bit-identical results to before. Bounded, fused-bounded and `replacement_chain_schedule` kernels keep closure semantics.
- Checkpoints record the resolved `walks`, `step_scale`, `rwalk_proposal` and `jax_block_size`, so a resume with default arguments matches a fresh run with default arguments. Checkpoints without `rwalk_proposal` are read as `"isotropic"` (no format-version bump).
- The benchmark and validation harnesses (`bench_static.py`, `overnight_jax_validation.py`, `validation/run_validation.py`) pin `rwalk_proposal="isotropic"` and `jax_block_size=1` unless a block size is requested, so their configurations are unchanged.

## v0.1.0-alpha

- Narrowed TinyNS public sampler surface to `sample="rwalk"` and `sample="prior"`.
- Removed legacy `slice`, `rslice`, and `sample="bound"` public sampler paths.
- Documented the recommended unbounded isotropic JAX rwalk fast path with `jax_block_size=32`.
- Added cached JAX block rwalk validation summaries and release checklist commands.
- Added optional B16/B32/B64/B128 block-size smoke documentation; B32 remains the default recommendation.
- Added a self-contained 10D GW-like stress-test template with diagnostic figures.
- Added or documented robust block-mode termination behavior for late replacement failures.
- Added a `logzerr` diagnostics breakdown and additional rwalk/replacement telemetry (`repl_ncall`, `repl_chains`, rescue/usage stage counts) to results and metadata.
- Added `NestedSamplingResult.logz_bootstrap(...)` (exported `LogZBootstrap`), a simulated-weights evidence-uncertainty estimator: it jitters the prior-volume shrinkages from their `Beta(nlive, 1)` law over the stored dead-point sequence and recomputes `logz` many times, returning the realization mean, standard deviation, and 16/50/84 percentiles. This captures the skewed prior-volume path uncertainty that the analytic `sqrt(H/nlive)` cannot. It is pure post-processing (no sampler changes) and does not capture sampling bias, so it is a lower bound on true single-run uncertainty when the constrained sampler is under-decorrelated (e.g. an inflated `insertion_rank_mean_z`).
- Added an experimental `rwalk_adaptive_step_scale` JAX-only rwalk option that adapts the isotropic proposal scale from constrained-replacement acceptance telemetry (default off, `step_scale=0.1` unchanged); the acceptance telemetry it adapts from was fixed to reflect true rwalk move acceptance rather than an earlier proxy.
- **Removed** `rwalk_proposal="live-cov"` and `rwalk_cov_jitter`: validation found concerning evidence pulls, and per-axis unit-cube reflection is not symmetry-preserving for correlated proposals. `rwalk_proposal` stays and now accepts only `"isotropic"`; passing anything else raises a clear removal error.
- **Removed** the `replacement_chain_schedule` + `jax_block_size > 1` combination: the in-JIT adaptive kernel evaluated all `max_chains` likelihoods at every stage regardless of schedule position, saving no compute while understating `ncall`/`chains_used` telemetry. Adaptive replacement-chain schedules remain supported at `jax_block_size=1` (the default).
- Fixed block-mode `ncall` undercounting failed-offset and rescue-ladder likelihood calls on an unrescued replacement failure; these are now always counted, matching per-iteration accounting.
- Fixed checkpoint cadence so `checkpoint_interval` is honored in block mode: checkpoints are now written on elapsed nested-iteration deltas rather than an exact-modulo check, so a large `jax_block_size` no longer skips the first several periodic saves (e.g. block 32 / interval 100 previously did not checkpoint before iteration 800).
- Fixed resume labeling: a resumed run that had already converged now reports `success=True, "converged"` instead of an incorrect `"maxiter reached"` message, and a resumed run at or past `maxiter` without convergence is labeled `"maxiter reached"` rather than defaulting to `"converged"`; fixed in both block and per-iteration modes.
- Persisted `effective_step_scale` in checkpoints as an optional `.npz` field (no checkpoint format-version bump; older checkpoints without the field still load) so adaptive step-scale state survives resume; added `rwalk_adaptive_step_scale`/`rwalk_target_accept` config validation on resume.
- Added a warning (not an error) when `NestedSampler` receives unknown keyword arguments; unknown kwargs are still stored, preserving dynesty drop-in compatibility.
- Validated `maxiter >= 1`; a fresh run with `maxiter=0` previously fell through and was mislabeled `"converged"`.
- Deduplicated run-loop state/bound-rebuild helpers, bound-rwalk JAX wrappers, and tuple-based ellipsoid-bound primitives; behavior-preserving, no public API change.
- Kept bounds, fused bounds, and larger block sizes experimental.

## Release caveats

TinyNS v0.1.0-alpha is intended as a small static nested sampler with a validated low-dimensional JAX rwalk fast path. High-dimensional, strongly curved, or multimodal targets require target-specific validation. The included 10D GW-like benchmark is a stress test for constrained-replacement mixing, not a production GW parameter-estimation pipeline.
