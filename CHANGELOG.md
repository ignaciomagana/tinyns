## v1.1.0 (2026-10-25)

1.1 works on the problem that 1.0.0 shipped unfixed: a mode can be lost with no warning (see *Known limitations* of v1.0.0). The sampler keeps thin modes more often (*Algorithm*), and `tinyns.compare(results)` flags the runs of a batch that lost a mode (*Added*). Fixed-seed results can differ from 1.0.0 only where the clustering splits, which includes curved unimodal targets that it cuts into pieces; a run whose clustering never splits is bit-identical (Gaussians in 2 to 16 dimensions and the eggbox at nlive 1000, float32 and x64). Checkpoints of 1.0.0 are refused (*Breaking changes*). The rule of PR #169 is folded into this change and that PR is superseded. Unless they say otherwise, the figures below are from batched runs on 4 CPU cores (JAX 0.10.2), with 1.0.0 (a3c77c4) and 1.1 (the sampler of cc81d24) on the same keys. Some 1.1 figures come from an earlier commit of the change, 4a24486, whose runs differ from those of cc81d24 only in the recorded `mode_history` (samples, likelihoods and labels were identical where compared), or from the prototype of the lost-mode study, which gave the same runs as cc81d24 on every cell checked (M18 at nlive 1000, the 4-D mixture at 500 and 1000, the 6-D mixture at 1000); the 6-D mixture's rates at nlive 2000 to 8000 are from the prototype alone.

### Algorithm: the hop keeps thin modes

- **Why modes were lost.** Once a mode is separated from the others, only the hop brings points into it. A new point is a walk from a live seed, so without the hop a mode's live count is a critical branching process (each point dies about once per e-fold of prior volume and seeds about one new point) in which 0 is absorbing: from `n0` points the mode dies out within `T` e-folds with probability about `(T / (1 + T))^n0`, and doubling `nlive` roughly squares that. The hop proposes only from *eligible* frames, and the 1.0.0 rule (a volume share that predicts `2 * ndim` live points, and more than `ndim` members) left out exactly the thin clusters that need it: a cluster whose share is below `2 * ndim / nlive` can never pass it, and the volume of a frame fitted to few points is biased low (by several nats for 20 to 30 points at d = 18). A frame also went with the last member of its cluster, after which nothing could bring the mode back. On `build_two_mode(18, 0.2)` of `bench.targets` (M18 below) the 20% mode was split off with 27 to 47 live points, lived for 15 to 20 e-folds as a cluster of 5 to 30, below that rule, and in some runs drifted to none.
- **Eligibility.** A cluster takes part in the hop from 3 members (`tinyns.modes.eligible_min_points(ndim) = min(3, ndim + 1)`: 2 in 1-D, which 1.0.0 admitted too), whatever its volume share; the most populated cluster still always does. Every cluster that 1.0.0 admitted is admitted, and so is every cluster that the rule of PR #169 admitted (more than `ndim` members and either the 1.0.0 volume share or `2 * ndim` members), which this replaces.
- **Kept frames.** A slot whose cluster falls below 3 members (2 in 1-D) at a recluster, or is emptied by a merge, keeps the last frame it had with at least that many (mean, Cholesky factor and its inverse, log-determinant) for `GHOST_RECLUSTERS = 8` reclusters, about two e-folds: active and eligible for the hop, not for the local walk (`tinyns.modes.keep_frames`). New points may take its label, so a mode that the hop refills is reclustered under its old id; a slot that holds 3 members again is an ordinary frame, and a split may take a kept slot that holds none. `State` gains `mode_ghost`, the countdown of each slot (`(K, C)` int32).
- **Exactness.** The hop is still independence Metropolis-Hastings with the exact ratio of proposal densities, and any fixed set of frames leaves the uniform law on `{L > L*}` invariant. The frames that drive a chain of fold `j` never read fold `j`'s points: a kept frame is one the same clustering fitted earlier to points outside fold `j`, and whether a slot keeps one reads that clustering's counts alone. `tests/test_thin_modes.py` checks that two live sets that differ only in fold 0 give clustering 0 the same frames to the bit, through a recluster with a thin cluster and one after it emptied, and that the hop with a kept frame keeps the constrained prior (z = -0.64 in float32, -0.63 in x64, with 35% of the chains changing ball; a control whose frame depends on the chain's fold gives z = -58). The validation ran the hop with kept frames as `core._step` runs it, 200,000 chains in each of four cases on disjoint balls in 3-D, in float32 and x64: a ball emptied of live points, so that its kept frames have no members; one left with 1 or 2; one whose two halves a recluster merged, so that the emptied slot's kept frame overlaps the merged live frame; and the two-frame kernel, with one live cluster and the kept frame of a second, emptied ball. Chi-square p 0.22 to 0.99 and every |z| at most 1.94; about 25,500 to 31,600 chains per case moved into the ball whose frame was kept, in all but the merge case (where that ball keeps its points in the merged cluster) only through the kept frame. A control whose kept frame was in the kernel only when the chain's seed lay in that ball failed at z = -162.

### Added: `tinyns.compare`

- **`tinyns.compare(results)`** returns a `tinyns.Comparison` for a list of results of one problem: the list that a batched run returns, runs of several calls, or results read by `load_npz`. A run is flagged if its logZ lies more than `COMPARE_SIGMA = 4` combined `logzerr` (`sqrt(e_best^2 + e_run^2)`) below the highest, or if it lacks a mode that another run found: a mode of mass at least `COMPARE_MIN_MASS = 0.05` in that run's `modes()`, of which this run holds less than `COMPARE_LOST_SHARE = 0.1`, or `COMPARE_LOST_SHARE_UNTRACKED = 0.01` for a mode that the other run's clustering did not separate (the hop did not balance it, and its mass scatters as without the hop). The constants are in `tinyns.result`. `Comparison` has `runs` (one dictionary per run: `run`, `flagged`, `reason`, `compared`, `logz`, `logzerr`, `nlive`, `nmodes`, `logz_shortfall`, `missing_modes`), `flagged`, `best` and `summary()`, which `print` shows. `compare` runs no likelihood and compiles nothing (checked with `jax.monitoring`). On 4 CPU cores a batch of 4 saved runs of M18 (x64, about 72,000 samples per run; 1.0.0 and 1.1 runs alike) took 0.7 to 0.9 s, and 0.2 s when compared again with each run's `modes()` cached; a batch of 4 runs of `mix3_d4` at nlive 500 took 0.1 s, and 128 eggbox runs at nlive 1000 compared at once took 103 s, about 40 s of it in `modes()`.
  - *Modes are matched by location, not counted.* This run's samples go to the nearest of the other run's modes whose frame reaches them: the posterior-weighted mean and covariance of the mode's samples, out to the `r^2` within which they hold all but a thousandth of its mass. A sample that no frame reaches counts for none. So the test does not depend on how this run's own modes were counted, which a count-based rule did: it flagged correct runs whose `modes()` had merged two modes, in 154 of 1638 batches without a lost run against 43 (`mix3_d4` at nlive 1000, saved 1.0.0 runs, batches of 4). With the reach, two runs that each lost a different mode are both flagged: 96 of 96 such runs, against 48 of 96 without it (runs constructed by removing a different mode's samples from each of two saved 1.0.0 runs of `mix3_d4` that kept every mode). A run that reports one mode is a reference too, in the frame of all its samples.
  - *Runs that cannot be compared.* A run that did not succeed (`maxiter`, `maxcall`, `invalid`), has no posterior weight or has a `logzerr` that is not finite is flagged "not compared", with the reason (the run's message, or that logZ or `logzerr` is not finite), is never the best, and takes no part in the comparison of the others. The lanes of `run(keys, batched_data=True)` are of different datasets and are refused with a `ValueError` (alone, mixed with other runs, and after `save_npz` and `load_npz`), as are runs of different `ndim`; a result passed alone, not in a list, raises `TypeError` (a list of one is accepted and compares nothing), and an empty list raises `ValueError`. Runs at different `nlive`, `walks` or `num_delete` can be compared. A result saved before 1.1, or made by `finalise` (only `run` records `batched_data`), is accepted, so the caller must make sure such runs share one likelihood and prior; results saved by 1.0.0 load and compare. Flags do not depend on the order of the runs.
  - *On saved 1.0.0 runs* (the lost-mode study, 2000 random batches of 4 per pool). `mix3_d4` at nlive 500 (13 of 64 runs lost the 0.6 mode): 1217 of the 1219 batches with a lost run flagged (the other 2 held only lost runs), none of the 781 without. At nlive 1000 (3 of 64 lost): 362 of 362, and 43 of the 1638 without, each of which held the one run that kept every mode but whose logZ lay 4.6 `logzerr` below the truth. M18 (11 of 128 lost, with logZ within 2 `logzerr` of the truth): 636 of 636 by the mode test, none of the 1364 without. Controls (32 runs each at nlive 1000: Gaussians in 8-D and 16-D, `sepW_d10`, W18, `rosen_d10` and the eggbox): no batch flagged in 2000 of each.
  - *On 1.1 runs* (consecutive batches of 4). `mix3_d4` at nlive 300 and 500, float32 and x64 (16 keys of 32 runs per cell, 2048 runs): 547 runs lost the 0.6 mode and 533 were flagged (529 by the mode test, 4 by the logZ test alone), and none of the 1501 that kept every mode. It missed none of the 181 lost at nlive 500. The 14 it missed at nlive 300 were 12 in batches whose every run had lost the mode, and 2 in a batch whose one run that kept it had merged it with the 0.3 mode in `modes()` (their logZ 2.6 and 3.8 combined `logzerr` low). On the same keys, the runs of 1.0.0 lost 704, and `compare` missed 38 (36 in all-lost batches, 2 against a merged reference) and flagged 2 runs that kept every mode. M18 (64 runs on keys 751 and 752, x64): it flagged the one lost run and no other. The 6-D mixture (48 runs at nlive 1000, 40 lost): it flagged the 16 lost runs that shared a batch with a run that kept the mode, and none of the 24 in the 6 batches that had all lost it. Controls (a Gaussian in 8-D, `sepW_d10`, W18, `rosen_d10` and the eggbox, 64 runs each at nlive 1000): no run that kept every mode was flagged, in consecutive batches, in 2000 random batches or in the pool of 64. On the eggbox 17 runs lost a peak (below a tenth of its mass); such a peak (2% or 4% of the posterior) clears the 0.05 bar only where another run put it above it, and `compare` flagged 2 of the 17 in consecutive batches of 4 and 14 with all 64 runs at once. On the targets of #169 (`cauchy_d18`, `needle_d18`, `sepW_d18`, `sepM_d10`, `connW_d32`) it flagged none of 383 runs that kept the mode, and missed the one `connW_d32` run that lost it: `modes()` reported one mode in all 32 runs of that cell on 1.1 (and in every run of 1.0.0 and #169).
  - *Limits.* A mode that every run of a batch lost is not found: with a loss rate `p`, a batch of `B` runs misses it with probability `p^B`. Nor is a mode that no run of the batch separated in `modes()`. A larger batch, or a higher `nlive`, makes the first rarer; the second only where some run separates the mode, which none of the 96 `connW_d32` runs did (see *Known limitations*). But in a large batch the highest logZ is high by chance, so the logZ test can flag a correct run: in batches of 32 of the 2048 runs of 1.1 above it flagged 8 of the 1501 that kept every mode (4.1 to 4.7 combined `logzerr` low), and none in batches of 4. The bars sit close to the scatter of correct runs: on 1.0.0, a run that kept M18's 20% mode held at least 0.13 of the mass another run found there (26,912 pairs; the bar is 0.1), and on the eggbox, which the hop does not balance, a run that kept every peak held at least 0.021 of a peak's mass (89,280 pairs of 128 runs at nlive 1000; the bar is 0.01). On 1.1 runs the margins are wider: at least 0.86 on M18 and 0.56 on `mix3_d4` at nlive 500 (0.24 to 0.36 on 1.0.0). A target whose modes scatter more from run to run can get a correct run flagged, the more likely the larger the batch. No fallback test was added: on 1.1 the misses were 2.6% of the lost runs (4.3% in the worst cell, nlive 300 in float32), and 12 of the 14 were in batches where no run held the mode.

### Changed

- **`result.metadata["batched_data"]`** (bool) says whether the run was a lane of `run(keys, batched_data=True)`; `compare` reads it. Results saved before 1.1 lack it, and so do those of `finalise`. It joins the metadata keys listed under v1.0.0.
- **Advice.** The unresolved-mode warning of `diagnostics()` now ends "raise nlive and compare runs (tinyns.compare)", and the docstrings of `modes()` and `compare()` give the loss rates of 1.1, each figure labelled with its sampler. The `modes()` docstring also says that `run_sd` falls short where the hop balances the modes only in part (the needle figures above).
- **`metadata["mode_history"]`.** The clusters column counts a kept frame only while its slot holds members (as 1.0.0 counted such a slot), and the eligible column counts every kept frame. So the history shows the hop on for longer once a mode has died out: on the heavy-and-tall pair of `tests/test_modes.py` (nlive 500, 25 runs), the share of the posterior mass with the hop off, measured to the end of the run (`diagnostics()` does not report it), fell from 0.55–0.63 (1.0.0, x64) to 0.09–0.15 (1.1, float32 and x64), because the dead mode's kept frame stays eligible. `diagnostics()["mode_hop_off"]` averages only while two modes hold live points (the whole run for one mode), and on that pair it was at most 0.033 on 1.0.0 and 0.001 on 1.1.

### Fixed

- **`modes()` printed numpy warnings** ("Mean of empty slice", "invalid value encountered in divide") when, of two sets of live points it compares, one held only copies of points of the other; seen on 1.1 runs of the needle targets. Its result was right (no direction, no gap) and is unchanged.

### Breaking changes

- **Checkpoint format `tinyns-ckpt-8`.** `State` gains `mode_ghost`. A `tinyns-ckpt-7` checkpoint (1.0.0) is refused with a `ValueError` that names its format, and there is no reader: finish a run on the version it started on. Result files keep their format (`tinyns-result-npz-v4`), and those of 1.0.0 load.
- **`State` has one more field**, so code that builds a `State` by position needs it; `init` builds it.
- **Private API.** `tinyns.modes.frames(st, hop=False)` replaces `frames(st, nlive=None)`. `ELIGIBLE_PER_DIM` is gone; `ELIGIBLE_MIN_POINTS`, `GHOST_RECLUSTERS`, `eligible_min_points` and `keep_frames` are new.
- **Exports** gain `compare` and `Comparison`.

### Known limitations

- **A mode that holds 1 or 2 live points when its contour separates is lost in most runs**, with logZ about `log(1 - mass)` low: the clustering rarely splits it off with enough points to be kept. A mode's live count when it separates is `nlive` times its share of that contour's prior volume, not of the posterior, and it needs about 5 live points or more there to be split off and kept. The 1.1 rules did not change that: the 6-D mixture above (1.7 of 1000 there) lost its 0.6 mode in 40 of 48 runs at nlive 1000 on 1.1 and 39 on 1.0.0, and in 3 of 16 at nlive 8000 on the study's prototype (4 on 1.0.0). Only more live points help, and by the extinction law above doubling `nlive` roughly squares the odds of a loss. Run 4 seeds of one problem and use `tinyns.compare`, which flags the lost runs of a batch that holds a run that kept the mode and separated it in `modes()`.
- **Thin modes are still lost.** The 4-D mixture (about 6 of 500 live points at the separation) lost its 0.6 mode on 1.1 in 88 of 512 runs (17%) at nlive 500 and 8 of 512 (1.6%) at nlive 1000, with no warning in nearly every case.
- **A mode that `modes()` does not separate** can be lost unseen, and `compare` cannot flag it: on `connW_d32` (two connected modes, nlive 1000, float32) `modes()` reported one mode in every run, and 1.1 lost the 6% mode in 1 of 32 runs with no warning (logZ 0.03 below the truth, `logzerr` 0.35).
- **The minor mass of the needle targets scatters more than on 1.0.0**, by about 10% in logit sd on average and up to a quarter, at nlive 2000 as well as 1000, and `run_sd` understates its scatter (see *Algorithm*).
- **A likelihood call costs up to 9% more on a CPU** on the lanes of W18 that 1.1 changed, and up to 2% more on a GPU on M18 (W18 was not timed on a GPU; see *Algorithm*). *Corrected after the release (2026-10-26):* on a GPU the cost is 3 to 6% in x64, and up to 17% on W18 in x64 in batches of 64 (see *Unreleased*).

## v1.0.0 (2026-10-24)

tinyns v1 is a rewrite: a new full-JAX core, a new algorithm and a new API.
**It is a hard break from v0.x.** There are no compatibility shims, no
deprecated aliases and no readers for v0.x checkpoints or result files; no
result is bit-identical to v0.x. Finish in-flight runs on the version they
started on. The per-PR development notes that this entry replaces are in the
git history of this file (up to commit eb4631c). A first `v1.0.0` tag, at
8851058 (2026-10-12), was reviewed before any upload. The fixes that review
led to are listed under *Fixed before release* or, where they change a
behaviour described below, in that description; fixed-seed results differ
from 8851058 wherever those fixes reach. One problem the review found ships
unfixed: a mode can be lost with no warning (see *Known limitations*).

### Highlights

- **The whole run executes on the device.** A compiled `lax.while_loop` runs chunks of steps on the GPU or CPU; the host syncs once per chunk.
- **Speed.** On one NVIDIA H100 in float32, a 13-D correlated Gaussian with 1000 live points runs to `dlogz = 0.1` in 0.93 s once compiled and 9.8 s on a first run (about 8 s of it compilation), against 47 s and 53 s for v0.2.5: 50x compiled, 5x with compilation. At 4000 live points it is 1.15 s (10.3 s) against 227 s (233 s): 200x and 23x. With `num_delete=1` v1 takes 35 s and 136 s compiled, 1.3x and 1.7x faster than v0.2.5. A two-mode 10-D target took 0.91 s compiled against 0.66 s for a 10-D Gaussian (15 to 20% more time per likelihood call with two modes and the hop active; the Gaussian pays for the mode tracking too, see *Known limitations*). Measured on the final v1 core (d638c55), which is slower than the first v1 core (0.56 s on the same target) because of the mode tracking, and before the fixes of the review, which were not timed on the GPU. For a cheap likelihood the run time on a GPU is nearly flat in `nlive`, because the number of steps does not depend on `nlive` when `num_delete` is proportional to it.
- **Multimodal posteriors are handled inside the sampler.** An exact inter-mode move cut the seed-to-seed scatter of minor-mode weights about 7x against chains seeded from live points alone, at the same number of likelihood calls (v1 bake-off: Gaussian mixtures of two and three modes in 4 to 32 dimensions, 40 seeds per cell, H100).
- **Batched runs.** `run(keys)` runs a batch of independent runs in one compiled program; with `batched_data=True` each run has its own dataset (SBC and mock campaigns compile once).
- **Checkpoints.** `run(key, checkpoint=path)` writes and auto-resumes; on a CPU a resumed run is bit-identical to an uninterrupted one; on a GPU in float32, where a fixed seed is not bit-reproducible between processes, the two agree in distribution.
- **A functional core** (`Config`, `init`, `step`, `finalise`) next to the `NestedSampler` class.

### Algorithm

- **Step.** Each step deletes the `k = num_delete` lowest live points and replaces them with the end points of `k` fixed-length Metropolis chains, vmapped. A chain is seeded from a live point above the new contour `L*`; proposals outside the open unit cube or at or below `L*` are rejected (no reflection), and a chain that never moves returns its seed (no retry rule). With `num_delete=1` the chain is not vmapped, so a proposal rejected without the likelihood (outside the cube, or by a hop's or local walk's Hastings test) skips the call. A batched run vmaps that chain over the runs and evaluates such proposals anyway, uncounted: `ncall` counts the calls the run would make alone, and `maxcall` limits that count.
- **On-device clustering** (`tinyns/modes.py`). Every quarter e-fold of prior volume the live points are reclustered in 8 fixed slots: warm-started hard EM, merges, and top-down Fisher splits. Every cluster has an id: the two parts of a split take new ids and the id they were split from is recorded as their parent, so an id names one cluster from the split that made it to the split that ends it (a merge keeps the id of the cluster that absorbs the other).
- **Local random walk.** A walk step is `x + s L_c z`, with `L_c` the Cholesky factor of the covariance of the cluster of `x`, and the exact Hastings ratio when the proposal lands in another cluster. With one cluster it is a walk in the live-point covariance. The scale `s` adapts toward 25% acceptance after every step.
- **Hop.** Every 10th chain step is an independence Metropolis-Hastings proposal from the uniform law on the union of the clusters' ellipsoids (the bake-off arm `B_ell`), accepted with the ratio of proposal densities. With fewer than two eligible clusters it is a walk step.
- **Cross-fitting.** The live slots form three folds. A new point is seeded from the fold of the slot it fills, and its chain's clusters and covariances are fitted to the other two folds only. The kernel therefore does not depend on the seed or its ancestors, and it leaves the uniform law on `{L > L*}` invariant for any chain length. `modes.py` gives the argument.
- **Default `walks`** is `max(25, 6 * ndim, ndim**2 // 6)` (`tinyns.core.default_walks`). It keeps the logZ bias below its scatter on correlated Gaussians from 2 to 64 dimensions with 250 to 2000 live points, which holds for posteriors that are roughly Gaussian in the unit cube. A nonlinear prior transform that presses most coordinates against faces of the cube needs more: `ndtri_d6` of `bench/validate.py` needs 4 times as many (see *Known limitations*).
- **Evidence.** The `k` deaths of a step are counted at live counts `m, ..., m-k+1` (birth/death contours; Fowlie, Handley & Su) and the final live points at `m, ..., 1`, except those still tied at the last step's contour, which count on from the tied deaths (`m - j - k * tie_run`). `finalise` recomputes the weights, `logz` and `logzerr`, the first-order spread of `logz` over the prior-volume path (`logzerr^2 = sum_j (S_j / n_j)^2`, `S_j = d logz / d log t_j = sum_{i>j} p_i - L_j X_j / Z`), on the host in float64 from the live count each dead row records (`Dead.nlive`). `result.nlive_i` holds the live count at every death. The sum of information increments that `logzerr` replaces (`sqrt(sum_i dH_i / n_i)`) dropped the volume uncertainty of zero-weight tied deaths and went negative, clipped to 0, on a flat final live set: an indicator likelihood got `logzerr` 0.000 in every run. Over 128 runs `sd(logz) / logzerr` is 1.00-1.28 on 2-D Gaussians and 1.02-1.07 on a 2-D flat top (1.11-1.79 with the old sum). Without ties `logzerr` moves by -3% (10-D) to about +11% (2-D), and by up to about +17% at very small nlive or low information; `logz` and the samples are unchanged. Unless they say otherwise, the `logzerr` figures elsewhere in this entry (in *Defaults*, *Known limitations* and *Benchmarks* in particular) were measured with the old sum.
- **Plateaus (exact likelihood ties).** A region where the likelihood is `-inf` or NaN counts as prior volume with zero likelihood, so `logz` is the evidence over the full prior. New points land strictly above `L*`, so live points tied at one likelihood (such a region, a finite plateau, a floor) die over several steps while the new points sit above them. The `q` tied deaths now count at `m, m-1, ..., m-q+1` (Fowlie, Handley & Su 2021): death `j` of a step whose likelihood equals the previous step's contour counts at `m - j - k * r`, with `r` the consecutive earlier steps at that contour. Before, every step was credited the compression of `m` live points, which under-compressed the prior volume by `-ln(1 - p) - p` (`p`: the tied fraction) and put that into logZ, whatever `nlive`, `num_delete` or `walks`. `State` carries the bookkeeping (`log_x_ties`, `lstar_prev`, `tie_run`; the log prior volume is `-it * S + log_x_ties`, so `-it * S` stays exact in float32), so the running evidence, `delta_logz`, the stopping rule, the progress line and the functional core count it too. Measured on CPU, a 5-D Gaussian (logZ = 0) with zero likelihood on half the cube, 48 batched runs at nlive 500: logZ +0.193 ± 0.017 before and +0.010 ± 0.018 with the plateau counts at `num_delete=25` (-0.007 ± 0.018 at the release, JAX 0.10.2), +0.169 ± 0.015 and -0.001 ± 0.016 at 50, and +0.174 ± 0.025 and -0.021 ± 0.027 at 1 (32 runs); a NaN region gives the same runs as a `-inf` one. On a gravitational-wave population likelihood that is zero on 52% of its prior, 6 runs at `num_delete=25` gave -192.89 against a truth of -193.15; their recount with the plateau counts gives -193.11 ± 0.08 (2 runs at `num_delete=1`: -192.98 before, -193.17 recounted). A run without ties is bit-identical to before (samples and logz, CPU, x64 on and off). The only exact ties in a run without a plateau are the rare copies of an unmoved chain's seed that die in consecutive steps (a few rows per run, or float32 rounding): they now count at `m - j - k`, which moved logz by at most 4e-4 on `sepW_d10`. A run that ends on a plateau (an indicator or step likelihood) leaves the last `r < k` tied points live, because the step that would delete them reaches the top level and finds nothing above it; they now count on from the tied deaths, as they do when dlogz, maxiter or maxcall stops a run during a tie run. Counted at `m, ..., m-r+1`, they put logZ `log((m - kT)(m - r) / (m (m - kT - r)))` too high (`T`: the tie run): +0.134 on a 2-D disc of area 0.25 at the defaults, +0.66 at area 0.05 with nlive 200, +0.199 for a two-level likelihood. Now +0.009 ± 0.010, -0.037 ± 0.061 and +0.011 ± 0.006 (32 runs each). `State.lstar_prev` starts at `-inf` (it was NaN), so runs work under `jax_debug_nans`, and so does `diagnostics()` on a likelihood with `-inf` regions (the information no longer forms `0 * -inf`). The checkpoint format is unchanged (`tinyns-ckpt-7`).
- **Termination.** The run stops when `log(Z + X max L_live) - log Z < dlogz`, at `maxiter` dead points, at `maxcall` likelihood calls, on a likelihood plateau (no live point above the deleted ones), or at a likelihood of `+inf`: the first step after which a live point has a likelihood of `+inf` sets the new status `"invalid"` (per lane in a batch; the functional core sets it too), with `success=False`. A run whose live points have no finite likelihood (all `-inf` or NaN), or whose live points above the deleted ones are all `+inf`, is `"invalid"` from its first step, so the progress line (`[invalid]`, or `invalid=1` for a lane of a batch), the checkpoint and the functional core's `state.status` agree with the result; they said `"plateau"`.

### API

- **Sampler.** `NestedSampler(loglike, prior_transform, ndim, nlive=1000, *, num_delete=None, walks=None)`, with `num_delete` defaulting to `max(1, nlive // 10)` (at most `nlive // 2`). `loglike` and `prior_transform` are JAX-traceable functions of one point. Unknown keywords raise `TypeError`. `prior_transform` returns one array of any shape (a scalar at `ndim=1`, derived parameters), and it is only called on the open unit cube `(0, 1)^ndim`. `init` and `run` raise `TypeError` for a dict or tuple before sampling; before, the run sampled to the end and then `finalise` crashed or, for some sample counts, scrambled the samples. `Config` (so `NestedSampler`) issues a `UserWarning` below `1.5 * (ndim + 1)` live points, where the cross-fitted walk covariances cannot span every direction, pointing at the line that built it (a 32-D Gaussian gave logZ 16 to 19 nats high at nlive 20 and 9 at 40, with no warning).
- **Run.** `sampler.run(key, *, dlogz=0.1, maxiter=None, maxcall=None, progress=False, checkpoint=None, batched_data=False)`. `key` is a PRNG key (typed or raw), an int seed, or a batch of keys. `progress=True` prints one line per chunk. For a batch of runs the line gives the number running, the range of dead points, the largest dlogz and the median step scale over the running runs (left out once none runs), the calls of all runs, and the number of stopped runs in each status (`[converged=3 plateau=1]`); a run that stopped early no longer pins them.
- **Int seeds.** Every public function that takes a key takes an int seed too: `run`, `init`, `result.resample_equal` and `tinyns.math.systematic_resample`.
- **Functional core.** `Config(ndim, nlive, num_delete, walks)`, `init(key, loglike, prior_transform, cfg)`, `step(state, loglike, prior_transform, cfg) -> (state, dead)` and `finalise(state, dead, cfg, prior_transform=..., dlogz=..., ncall=...)`. `State` and `Dead` are pytrees of arrays. `tinyns.core` also has `default_walks` and the integer status codes. `finalise` raises `ValueError` unless `dead` holds the rows of exactly `state.it` steps: a step on a plateau returns the live points and `it` as they were and rows that are not valid (its chains still run: the key advances and `ncall` counts their calls, so a `lax.scan` chunk that goes on past a plateau keeps calling the likelihood). `examples/functional_core.py` keeps the rows of the first `state.it` steps (it passed the rows of a plateau chunk, and logZ of a box indicator came out -4.6 for a truth of -2.08), and the README loop stops on a status other than `"running"` (it never ended on a plateau). `finalise` maps the samples through `prior_transform` in blocks of the power of two at or above `num_delete`, at least 1024 rows (it was 65536 whatever the run), and halves a block that runs out of device memory: a heavy prior that sampled within memory no longer fails at the very end of the run or on resuming its final checkpoint. A plain function or closure is cached by identity: in an eager `step` loop, and for each new `NestedSampler`, a new object compiles again. A bound method is a new object at each access, so store it once (documented in `step` and the README; caching by equality would reuse a kernel after the instance changed).
- **Hand-written loops.** `tinyns.delta_logz(state, cfg)` is the stopping statistic of `run`, and `tinyns.STATUS` names the status codes (`STATUS[int(state.status)]`). `step` does not test termination, so `finalise(..., dlogz=)` applies the rule of `run`: a running state with `delta_logz < dlogz` is reported as converged (`success=True`); without `dlogz` the result says `running`. `State.ncall` is an int32, which wraps after `2**31` likelihood calls: `run` drains it, and a long hand-written loop adds up its increments on the host and passes `finalise(..., ncall=)`, as `examples/functional_core.py` does. Under `jax.vmap`, `step` runs all three chain kernels (three times the likelihood evaluations) and computes a recluster at every step, because the kernel choice and the recluster test are batched; a batch of runs belongs in `run`, which keeps both unbatched.
- **Exports:** `NestedSampler`, `Config`, `init`, `step`, `finalise`, `delta_logz`, `STATUS`, `NestedSamplingResult`, `LogZBootstrap`.
- **Result.** Fields: `samples`, `samples_u`, `logl`, `logwt`, `logl_birth`, `nlive_i`, `labels` (the id of each sample's cluster when it died, as `id * folds + clustering`), `insertion_ranks` and `insertion_slots` (the in-sampler rank of each new point and its number of possible ranks), `logz`, `logzerr`, `ncall`, `niter`, `nlive`, `num_delete`, `ndim`, `success`, `message`, `metadata`. Methods: `summary()`, `diagnostics()`, `weights()`, `resample_equal()`, `modes()`, `insertion_indices()`, `insertion_test()`, `logz_bootstrap()`, `information()`, `save_npz()`/`load_npz()` (format `tinyns-result-npz-v4`; earlier formats are refused), `to_dict()` and `to_numpy()` (every field, `labels` and the insertion ranks included), `to_dynesty_dict()`. `tinyns.STATUS` gains `"invalid"`. A result whose `logz` is not finite (`+inf`, or `-inf` when no finite likelihood was found) has the status `"invalid"`, `success=False` and a message naming the cause, also issued as a `RuntimeWarning`; `run()` does not raise, so one bad lane does not abort a batch. `diagnostics()` of such a run leads with that message and gives no advice on `dlogz` or `nlive`. A result without posterior weight (`logz = -inf`) has `posterior_ess()` 0, NaN weight fractions in `diagnostics()` and one mode; `summary()` and `print(result)` work, and the weight accessors (`weights()`, `log_weights()`, `max_weight_fraction()`, `live_weight_fraction()`, `dead_weight_fraction()`) and `resample_equal()` raise a `ValueError` with the run's message. Before, both ended with `success=True` on a "plateau", and after a run without a finite likelihood `summary()`, `print(result)` and `diagnostics()` raised.
- **`result.modes()`** reads the cluster labels the run recorded and returns each mode's mass, its run-to-run scatter measured within the run (`run_sd`, see *Within-run scatter*), the scatter without the hop (`urn_sd`: an upper bound for a mode the hop balanced, and right for two modes or modes of one shape only), its smallest live count, `extinction_iteration` (the first iteration from which the mode holds no live point; `niter` for a mode alive at the end), an `unresolved` flag (fewer than `5 * ndim` live points) and a `tracked` flag (`False`: the sampler's clustering did not separate the mode, see below). `summary()` prints the table when there is more than one mode, with the columns `run sd`, `fold sd` and `sd w/o hop` (`urn_sd`, which it labelled `logit sd`), and `diagnostics()` adds a warning per unresolved mode; with several modes it says that in other runs such a mode may be lost entirely. A unimodal run is flagged `unresolved` too when `nlive < 5 * ndim` (it never was: a 32-D Gaussian at nlive 20 gave logZ 16 to 19 nats high with no warning), and `summary()` prints that warning. `modes()` is worked out once per result: `diagnostics()`, `summary()` and `print(result)` reuse it, and each call returns a copy. Change a result with `dataclasses.replace`, not in place. Its nearest-neighbour search takes its distances in blocks: for a mode found by one clustering only it built an `nlive^2 * ndim` array, 6.2 GB at nlive 8000 in 18-D.
  - *Counting.* Labels that touch are merged (a gap of less than 3 within-cluster standard deviations along their discriminant, at one of their posterior medians). Three kinds of label do not merge the modes they touch: a cluster whose parts ended up apart (the recorded parents say which clusters were split into which), a cluster of another clustering that covers two modes (its own live points leave a gap between them), and an early cluster, which has no live points left at the median of the labels it touches (modes that are separate in the posterior bulk are still connected at a low likelihood contour). Before this, a cluster kept its label when it was split, so the label of a cluster that was split in the posterior bulk held several modes and merged them all: on the 10-D LogGamma target (4 modes of mass 1/4) 1 of 64 runs reported 4 modes at nlive 1000 and none at nlive 500, although the samples had all four with the right masses; now 64 and 63 of 64 do.
  - *Modes the clustering does not separate.* A mode is also cut wherever its live points leave a slab across a coordinate axis of the unit cube empty (width times the density of the 8 neighbouring points at least 30; the largest value over 768 runs of unimodal targets was 21.5). The modes this finds are `tracked: False`: the hop did not balance them, their masses scatter as without the hop (by at least about `run_sd`), and their number is a lower bound. On the eggbox (18 modes, on a lattice that no two-way split separates) `modes()` reported one mode and no warning; it now reports 13 to 18 at nlive 1000 (192 runs), none tracked, with `urn_sd` of 0.40 and 0.57 for the modes of mass 0.08 and 0.04, whose masses scatter by 0.38 and 0.61 in logit over 64 seeds (median `run_sd` 0.31 and 0.45 against a scatter of 0.38 and 0.50 over 16 CPU runs).
  - *Full slots.* `diagnostics()["mode_slots_full"]` is the fraction of the run during which all 8 cluster slots were in use. With more than one mode found it is a warning: no further mode could be split off then. It says that there may be more modes only when the clustering held no more clusters than the modes found; with more, the slots held pieces of the modes found (on an 18-D posterior with 2 modes the slots were full with 8 clusters, and the old warning was a false alarm).
  - *Within-run scatter.* Each mode has `fold_mass`, its mass among the samples of each of the three cross-fitted folds (a sample's fold follows from its label), and `fold_sd`, the standard deviation of its logit over the folds. A point and its descendants stay in one fold, so the folds sample the modes like three runs, and for modes of about one height the run's mass scatters from run to run by about `fold_sd / sqrt(3)`: 0.024 against a measured 0.019 on a plain two-mode mixture in 18-D, 0.06 against 0.06 on `sepW_d10` and `sepW_d18`, 0.24 against 0.19 on `needle_d18`, 0.15 against 0.32 on `cauchy_d18` and 0.6 against 0.25 (3 runs) on an 18-D posterior with a funnel neck. Every fold weighs its samples with the same prior volumes, so `fold_sd` misses their error, which moves all the folds' masses together. It vanishes for modes of one height and dominates when the heaviest mode is also the narrowest (4-D mixture of masses 0.75/0.2/0.05 and widths 0.25/0.5/0.75: a scatter of 0.056/0.053/0.079 in logit against 0.015/0.012/0.027 for `fold_sd / sqrt(3)`). Each mode's `run_sd = sqrt(fold_sd**2 / 3 + V)` adds that error, propagated to first order from the shrinkage of every death (median 0.053/0.046/0.065 there); `summary()` and the hop warning print it. - **`result.insertion_test()`** tests the rank of each new point among the survivors of its step strictly above `L*` (`nlive - num_delete` of them without ties) for uniformity, pooled and in windows. The sampler records the ranks and their slot counts (`Dead.insertion`, `Dead.nslots`; `result.insertion_ranks`, `result.insertion_slots`): replaying the births from `logl_birth` is ambiguous on a plateau, where initial points and new points share the birth contour. Survivors with exactly the new point's likelihood (a plateau above `L*`, the seed of an unmoved chain) take a random order with it: the rank is a uniform place among them. Each rank is mapped to `(rank + U) / slots` with `U` uniform (from a fixed seed) and tested against the uniform law, so rows with different slot counts pool. On the finite plateau below, the first window's median p-value went from 7e-268 to 0.44. On the half-cube toy above the first window's p-value went from 1e-27 to 0.34 (median of 48 runs); on the gravitational-wave likelihood the rank excess sat entirely in the first 2-2.5% of the deaths, where `L* = -inf`.
- **`result.diagnostics()`** warns when the walks are far too short: when 30% of the replacement chains or more returned their seed (`metadata["unmoved_fraction"]`, threshold `tinyns.result.UNMOVED_WARN`). The seed copies keep the insertion ranks uniform, so the insertion test flags only 4 to 25% of such runs, while logZ scatters 1.2 to 3.8 times `logzerr` (5-D and 10-D Gaussians at 4 to 1 walks) and is biased high in 16-D. At the default walks and nlive 500 the fraction was at most 0.07 on the targets of `bench` (the eggbox); the eggbox at nlive 100 comes close (0.25 to 0.28) and warns in 16 to 31% of runs. The warning catches gross shortfalls only (up to 1.5 times `logzerr` of scatter was measured at 0.12 to 0.27 unmoved); rerunning with twice the walks remains the check. On a run that ended on a plateau `diagnostics()` skips the final-live-weight warnings and the `final_delta_logz` check, and adds one note with the plateau's posterior mass and the advice to raise `nlive` if a higher region could have been missed. Before, a correct plateau stop got three warnings, one telling the user to tighten `dlogz`, which does not change such a run.
- **`result.metadata` keys:** `status`, `num_delete`, `walks`, `dlogz`, `maxiter`, `maxcall`, `final_delta_logz`, `acceptance`, `unmoved_fraction`, `scale`, `ncall_valid` (the proposals that passed the likelihood-free tests: inside the cube and, for a hop or local-walk step, its Hastings test), `x64`, `hops`, `hop_tries`, `hop_acceptance`, `mode_history`, `mode_tree` (`[label, parent label]` of every cluster made by a split, up to 511 ids per clustering), `mode_tree_dropped` (the number of clusters made past those, whose parents are not recorded), `folds`, `chunks`, `wall_time_s`, `compile_s`, `sampling_s`, `resumed`.
- **Checkpoints** (`tinyns/checkpoint.py`, format `tinyns-ckpt-7`: the state holds the cluster ids and their parents and the plateau bookkeeping, and every dead row its live count and insertion slots; `tinyns-ckpt-6` and earlier are refused, with no reader). One `.npz` file, written atomically at a chunk boundary at most every 10 minutes and at the end. Each step splits the key it carries, so the random stream depends on the step count only: on a CPU (and the same JAX build and device) a resumed run is bit-identical to an uninterrupted one (tested, x64 on and off); on a GPU in float32 a run resumed in a new process agrees with it in distribution. A checkpoint with a different config, key, batch, x64 flag or float dtype is refused with a `ValueError`, as is one with live or dead points whose likelihoods the run's `loglike` and `prior_transform` do not give back. A run stopped by `maxiter`, `maxcall` or `dlogz` continues under the new call's limits. The timings in `metadata` add up over sessions.
- **Batched runs.** `run(jax.random.split(key, B))` returns a list of `B` results. `step` is vmapped over the runs inside the loop, and a finished run is frozen while the others finish. The device still computes a frozen run, so a batch evaluates about `B` times the likelihood calls of its longest run; each result's `ncall` counts its own run's calls. With `batched_data=True` every array leaf of a pytree callable carries a leading axis of `B`. A batched run is not bit-identical to the run of its key alone (vmap changes XLA's summation order): in float64 they agree to roundoff, in float32 they agree in distribution.
- **Pytree callables.** The arrays of `jax.tree_util.Partial(fn, data)` (or any pytree callable) are arguments of the compiled kernels, so datasets of one shape share one compilation. NumPy floating and complex scalar leaves (`Partial(fn, data_i, sigmas[i])`) are traced like arrays, so a new value no longer compiles again (about 3 s per dataset on a CPU, 74 s on the GW likelihood). Python scalars and NumPy integers and booleans stay static. A NumPy float leaf is now a traced value: Python control flow or a shape on it raises; pass a Python float there. A `Partial` whose only array-like argument is a NumPy float scalar is now keyed on its structure, like any `Partial` with array arguments. Under `batched_data` such a scalar needs the batch axis like any array leaf; the error says to pass a shared value as a Python float. Closure constants of 4096 elements or more are hoisted to arguments too, and the kernel caches hold neither argument arrays nor hoisted constants. An array captured by the function of a `Partial` with array arguments (not one of its arguments), or used by a closure inside an inner `jax.jit` or a `custom_jvp` rule, is embedded in the compiled program instead and held by the kernel caches until they evict it; pass shared arrays as `Partial` arguments (documented). The v0.2.3 note "a dataset is freed once you drop it" has these exceptions on every JAX version.
- **Precision.** x64 on and off are both supported and tested in CI.
- **Requirements.** Python 3.10+, `jax>=0.4.31`. CI runs the suite on Python 3.10 to 3.15: on JAX 0.4.31 (the floor) and 0.6.2 on Python 3.10, on 0.10.2 on 3.11 (the newest JAX for each), on 0.11.2 on 3.13, and on the newest JAX on 3.12 to 3.15 (JAX 0.11 and later need Python 3.12), with x64 on and off on 3.10, 3.11 and 3.13; the x64 job on JAX 0.4.31 runs NumPy 1.26.4 (pyproject allows NumPy 1.x, which pip does not pick there). One more job builds the sdist and the wheel, installs the wheel and runs the suite from the sdist against it, and CI also runs weekly. On JAX before 0.4.38 the first `run` raised `NotImplementedError: Batching rule for 'optimization_barrier' not implemented`: tinyns now registers that rule itself there (`tinyns/_compat.py`). One barrier is left, on the hop's normal draw (without it the first run compiles 2 s longer at 10 and 13 dimensions and 3 to 4 s longer at 32, on an H100); the three others changed neither the compiled run nor the compilation and are gone, so fixed-seed results differ from the previous v1 in roundoff.
- **Packaging.** The version is defined once, as `tinyns.__version__`; `pyproject.toml` reads it. The sdist ships only `src/`, `tests/`, `bench/`, `examples/`, the README, CHANGELOG, LICENSE, `pyproject.toml` and `.gitignore` (plus the generated `PKG-INFO`), whatever else is at the top of the working tree, and `make dist` builds the release files from `git archive HEAD`, so no untracked file ships. The license is the SPDX expression `MIT` (PEP 639; building needs hatchling 1.27 or later). `jaxlib` is no longer a direct dependency: `jax` pins its own. The classifiers say "5 - Production/Stable" (they said "3 - Alpha") and list Python 3.10 to 3.15, as CI runs them. `RELEASE_CHECKLIST.md` covers the build, inspection, tagging and upload steps.

### Fixed before release

The review of 8851058 found the problems below, fixed in this release. The other fixes are described with the behaviour they change, under *Algorithm*, *API*, *Benchmarks* and *Known limitations* (among them the evidence and `logzerr`, plateaus, invalid results, the prior transform's checks, the functional core, `modes()`, `diagnostics()`, NumPy float leaves of pytree callables, the CI matrix and the packaging).

- **Few points above L\*.** When fewer than `ndim + 1` points of the other folds lie above `L*`, the walk covariance now takes every point of the other folds, those at or below `L*` too. Before, it fell back to the points above `L*` of every fold (seeds included), lost its cross-fitting and went rank-deficient. This happens at the start of every run whose likelihood is `-inf` or tied on most of the prior. 10-D Gaussian on a box holding 1 to 10 of 500 initial points (volume noise removed): logZ came out +0.7 to +2.1 nats high (+0.25 at `num_delete=5`), several `logzerr`; it is now within -0.10 to +0.08 of the truth (seven sets of 8 to 16 runs on independent keys, float32 and x64; `logzerr` 0.19). 20-D at nlive 1000 with 2% support: +0.39 ± 0.12 -> -0.09 ± 0.07. On the 5-D floor of *Known limitations* (about 4 of 500 points above it) logZ went from +0.22 ± 0.05 to +0.11 ± 0.05 (96 runs). A run with `nlive=2` no longer stops on a false plateau after one step. Runs that always keep `ndim + 1` such points are bit-identical.
- **Open unit cube.** A float32 initial draw of exactly 0 (about one run in 500 at nlive 1000 in 16-D) gave ±inf samples under `ndtri` or `-log`, and NaN or -inf weighted means. A proposal that rounded to 1.0 was accepted. Initial draws of 0 now become `eps / 2`, the in-cube test is strict, and an out-of-cube proposal of a vmapped step (`num_delete > 1`, or any batched run) is evaluated at the chain's point, so `prior_transform` only sees `(0, 1)^ndim`. Runs that never touch a face give the same samples. With `num_delete > 1`, a run that often proposes outside the cube can differ in the last bit of its likelihood values.
- **float32 cluster frames.** The ridge that keeps cluster covariances positive definite is now relative to each axis (`10 eps (v_i + eps v)`) rather than to their mean variance. In float32 the old ridge made a frame 18 times too wide along an axis 1e-5 of the cube wide. That weakened the hop and the local walk, and it crushed the merge test, so modes close along a narrow axis were merged and re-split at every recluster. Results change slightly where the local walk or the hop runs (roundoff in x64, including some unimodal `num_delete=1` runs). float32 frame and Fisher-separation tests along a 1e-5 axis are in `tests/test_numerics.py`; the validation gate has no case anisotropic enough to see the ridge. The host ridge of `modes()` (float64) is relative to each axis too (`1e-12 (v_i + 1e-12 v)`). The old one swamped an axis below about 1e-6 of the others' width, which an x64 run resolves: two clusters 6 sd apart along an axis 1e-7 wide kept a quarter of their Fisher separation. It does not touch the run, and `modes()` gave the same output as before on Gaussian, two-mode and batched runs in float32 and x64.
- **TF32.** All internal matrix products ask for `lax.Precision.HIGHEST`. JAX's default TF32 on A100/H100 no longer rounds the means of narrow clusters (taken over raw cube coordinates), or adds 4e-3 to 9e-3 nats of noise to the local walk's Hastings ratios. Bit-identical on a CPU.
- **Strict promotion.** tinyns runs under `jax_numpy_rank_promotion="raise"` and `jax_numpy_dtype_promotion="strict"`; before, it raised in every run. Broadcasts and int/float casts are explicit, and integer indices are int32 under x64 too. A test runs a batched two-mode run, `num_delete=1` and the result methods under both flags in the float32 and x64 CI legs, JAX 0.4.31 included.
- **Seeding cost.** Drawing seeds with replacement and the insertion ranks now take one sort per step instead of `(num_delete, nlive)` masks (7.6 GiB per run at nlive 1e5), and the ranks' two binary searches are unrolled, so no sequential loop is left for a GPU. d = 4 on 4 CPU cores: a step at nlive 1e4 takes 7.8 ms instead of 44.8 ms, and a compiled run at nlive 4000 0.99 s instead of 1.32 s. Seeds, ranks and results are bit-identical.
- **Weights in float64.** `weights()`, `log_weights()`, `posterior_ess()`, `information()`, the weight entropy and the live/dead fractions, and `resample_equal()` compute in NumPy float64 on the host (`tinyns.math` is NumPy now and returns NumPy values). In float32 the normalisation put the weights off by about 3% and the information by 1.6% at |logL| ~ 1e6. And when the float32 weights summed below 1, `resample_equal` piled the excess onto the last sample (122 copies in 4000 draws where 2.3 were due). The weights now sum to 1 within ~1e-16, the CDF ends at exactly 1, and an index of zero weight is never drawn. `load_npz` returns NumPy arrays of the saved dtypes; without x64 it used to cast a float64 result to float32 and int64 to int32.
- **diagnostics() compiles nothing.** Every new sample count compiled 26-28 eager JAX ops, so `diagnostics()` took 0.7-0.85 s per lane of a batch; it now takes about 5 ms (CPU).
- **resample_equal order.** The draws come in a random order. Before, they came in death order (increasing likelihood), so a prefix or a split was biased: on a 0.1-sd 2-D target the first half had sd 0.127 and the second 0.055. The key is split between the systematic offset (a float32 draw, the same with x64 on and off) and the order, so fixed-seed draws differ from 8851058. The draws are not independent: each sample comes floor(n w) or ceil(n w) times. `n` must be an integer: `n=5.0` or `n=True` raises `TypeError`.
- **save_npz(path) writes exactly path**, atomically like a checkpoint. `np.savez_compressed` appended `.npz`, so `save_npz("result")` followed by `load_npz("result")` failed. A symbolic link at `path` is written through, and a file object is still accepted. The file is written next to `path` and renamed over it. So `save_npz` now needs write access to the directory, and it replaces a read-only file. Before, it raised `PermissionError` on a read-only file and could rewrite a file in a read-only directory. An `OSError` of `save_npz` or of a checkpoint save names the path, not the temporary file. The format is still `tinyns-result-npz-v4`.
- **A checkpoint of another problem is refused.** On resume the run evaluates again, in each lane with that lane's data, the 16 live points of highest likelihood and 16 dead points spread over the death order. It raises `ValueError` unless they give back the stored likelihoods: finite ones to a relative sqrt(eps) (about 3.5e-4 in float32), infinite ones exactly. The message gives the relative difference and whether the point was live or dead, and it names the switch that turns the check off (`tinyns.core._RESUME_CHECKS = 0`). Before, a checkpoint of another likelihood, prior or dataset with the same config and key was resumed: a mock loop that reused one path got the first dataset's result for every dataset. The dead points also catch a change the live points cannot see, such as a wider constraint of a finished plateau run or a change in a region the run has left. A change at none of the 32 points is not seen. In float32 a change below the tolerance is not seen either: a constant of 30 at `logL ~ -1e5`, and from about 1e5 data points possibly a new noise realisation of the same model (at 1e5 it was 1.2 times the tolerance). Use one path per dataset, or x64. This costs one small compilation and 32 likelihood calls per resume (0.2 s in a test on CPU; not counted in `ncall`).
- **The checkpoint path is checked first.** `run(checkpoint=path)` resolves `path` once (symbolic links and `..`). Before it compiles, it creates and removes a file next to the resolved path, and it raises `OSError` naming the path. A path that names no file (`""`, `"out/"`, `"x/.."`, a directory) raises `IsADirectoryError`. Before, a missing or unwritable directory, or such a path, failed at the first save, after a short run had computed everything. And `"missing/../run.npz"` was saved as `run.npz` but never resumed from it. A symbolic link at the path is followed: the linked file is replaced (before, the link itself was).
- **Checkpoints record the JAX build.** `meta_json` also holds `jax_version`, `jaxlib_version`, `threefry_partitionable`, and the platform (`backend`) and `device_kind` of the device the run's state is on. A resume under different values warns (a 0.4.34 checkpoint resumed under 0.10.2 matched neither uninterrupted run). A `mixed_build` flag keeps the warning at every later resume of that run. The format is still `tinyns-ckpt-7`.
- **Warnings name your line.** The `RuntimeWarning` of an invalid run and the checkpoint's warnings about another build (and `mixed_build`) name the line of your code that ran the sampler. They named a line of `tinyns/core.py` or `tinyns/api.py`, so Python's default filters showed each once per session, whatever line ran it.
- **File modes.** Checkpoints and result files are created with the mode of any new file (`0o666` less the umask; checkpoints were always 0600) and keep the mode of the file they replace, so a `chmod` that shares a checkpoint with a group survives its rewrites.
- A `.npy` file passed as the checkpoint raises `ValueError` like any other non-checkpoint (it raised `TypeError`). `Config` stores `_folds` as an int: an `np.int64` failed the JSON of the first save, at the end of a short run.
- **Host memory of long runs.** `run` copies each chunk's dead rows per run, where it used to keep a view: a view kept the chunk's whole buffer (every run of a batch, the full chunk capacity) alive until the end, which on a run without a checkpoint held hundreds of MB for a few MB of rows. Results are bit-identical.
- **Out of memory.** The `MemoryError` of `run` names what can shrink: `num_delete`, or the number of keys of a batched run. At `num_delete=1` it no longer suggests `num_delete=1`; it asks for less memory per likelihood call.
- **Closures under your own `jax.jit`.** A closure first used inside your own `jax.jit` (for instance `jax.jit(init)` over NumPy data) has its hoisted constants converted at trace time (`jax.ensure_compile_time_eval`). Before, the cached constant was a tracer of that trace, and every later use of the closure (an eager `step`, another jit or scan, a retrace, `finalise`, `run`) raised `UnexpectedTracerError`.
- **Datasets of finished runs on JAX >= 0.11.** The kernel caches no longer keep the dataset of a finished closure run alive: the cached jaxpr held its constants there (ClosedJaxpr and Jaxpr are one class since 0.11). Results are unchanged.
- **Static float leaves** are keyed on their bits: `0.0` and `-0.0` no longer share a compiled kernel, and a NaN leaf no longer compiles again for every new pytree.
- **Tests.** Stronger tests, no change to the sampler. `hop_accept`'s ratio of ellipsoid counts is tested on its own, including hops from a point in no ellipsoid, and on two frames whose ellipsoids overlap; the disjoint-mode gate never put a point in two ellipsoids. A test checks that each chain walks with the kernel of its seed's fold. The two-mode test asserts the scatter of the minor mass over 16 batched runs (sd below 0.03: 0.012 to 0.016 with the hop, 0.037 to 0.078 without), which its per-run window did not see. The thin-arms test runs 4 batched seeds per target and tolerates one needle run that lost a mode or much of one (one mode and no warning in 5 of 128 runs, or a minor mass outside 0.05 to 0.45: 0.466, with the warning, on JAX 0.4.31 in x64); runs of the plain control that lose their minor mode skip only its clusters-per-mode check. The 18-D test of modes that are not convex runs its plain control as its docstring describes, the same two modes without arms (`build_two_mode(18, 0.2, "sep", "W")`), and checks every run's oracle minor mass against the truth (within 0.03; within 0.011 in 120 runs on the release code, CPU). The old control (`build_two_mode(18, 0.2)`, shape M) lost its 20% mode with no warning in about 1 run in 8, in x64 as in float32, and the test, which averaged the masses of the runs that reported two modes, passed such runs. A `batched_data` run killed with its runs in mixed states (converged, plateau, running) resumes bit-identically (tested). The SBC example's 90% and 50% coverages are bounded on both sides ([0.80, 0.98] and [0.30, 0.65]). Two callables bit-identity tests were loosened: they failed on JAX 0.11.2 alone (a `Partial` prior against an embedded constant; an embedded 200k-element constant against a hoisted one). The first now gives both runs the same prior; the second checks the points and `ncall` exactly, and the likelihoods and `logz` to a relative 1e-6. A new test saves a result under x64 and loads it without x64, each in a new process.

### Defaults

- **`nlive=1000` and `num_delete = nlive // 10` are confirmed by a sweep; no code change (v1 PR 5).** 362 cells of 40 batched runs on an H100 (x64): `nlive` 250 to 4000 times `num_delete / nlive` 0.05, 0.1, 0.25 and 0.5 on Gaussians (d = 2, 10, 18, 32, 64), the mixtures sepW, connW and sepM (d = 10, 18, 32), mix3_d10, rosen_d10, funnel_d10 and the eggbox, plus `num_delete=1` at `nlive` 500 on two targets and 85 single-run timing cells. The tables, data and scripts are archived outside the repository. The README section "Choosing `nlive` and `num_delete`" gives the guidance. The sweep's `logzerr` is the sum of information increments (see *Algorithm*, Evidence).
  - *`num_delete`.* `logzerr^2 * ncall` is the same at every share to 2%, and the measured scatter agrees (pooled `(logz - truth) / logzerr` has sd 0.92 to 1.09 on the Gaussians at `nlive >= 500` and 0.92 to 1.08 on the mixtures, at every share): accuracy per likelihood call does not depend on `num_delete`. The run time does (single run of gauss_d10 at `nlive` 1000: 1.14, 0.71, 0.54 and 0.28 s at shares 0.05, 0.1, 0.25 and 0.5; 26.8 s at `num_delete=1`). Shares above 0.1 are not the default because the diagnostics degrade there: the eggbox's scatter over `logzerr` is 1.52 to 2.47 at 0.25 and 0.5 (0.89 to 1.24 at 0.1, 0.82 to 1.16 at 0.05), sepM_d10 at `nlive` 1000 has a logit sd of 0.06, 0.07, 0.22 and 0.50 at the four shares, gauss_d64 at `nlive` 250 is 1.75 +- 0.16 high at 0.5, and the pooled insertion test rejects 5.1%, 6.1%, 13.2% and 29.7% of the runs at the 5% level (3600 runs per share). `num_delete=1` used 8% (gauss_d18) and 17% (sepW_d18) fewer calls than the default with a 2% smaller `logzerr`.
  - *`nlive`.* At 1000 every Gaussian is unbiased up to 64 dimensions (at 250, d = 32 and 64 are 0.17 to 0.44 high), rosen_d10 is at -0.05 +- 0.04 (-0.16 +- 0.05 at 500, -0.42 +- 0.11 at 250, +0.00 +- 0.02 at 2000) and the single-run time is nearly flat (gauss_d10: 0.54 to 0.85 s from 250 to 4000; gauss_d32: 2.7 to 4.2 s), while the calls are proportional to `nlive`. The 6% mode has no lost seed, a logit bias within 3 se and a logit sd of at most 0.4 from `nlive` 500 / 500 / 1000 (sepW) and 500 / 500 / 2000 (connW) at d = 10 / 18 / 32, and 1000 / 2000 / 4000 for sepM (one seed of 40 lost at d = 32); a logit sd of at most 0.1 needs 1000 / 1000 / 2000 for sepW. A rule such as `max(1000, 60 * ndim)` would fix d = 32 for this mode weight and double to quadruple the calls of every unimodal run above 16 dimensions, so the default stays 1000 and the README gives `5 * ndim / f` to `10 * ndim / f`. That rule assumes that a mode's share of the live points follows its mass; it is its share of the prior volume when it separates, which for a narrow heavy mode was 1 to 2% in 4-D and under 1% in 6-D (see *Known limitations*).
  - *The unresolved flag misses most wrong weights.* The nine two-mode mixtures were run again at `num_delete = nlive // 10` with the `modes()` of 00176bb (36 cells of 40 runs; the same chains as the sweep at `nlive >= 1000`; `reflag.txt`). A run is wrong if its minor weight is lost or off by more than 0.4 in the logit: 178, 57, 16 and 1 of 360 runs at `nlive` 500, 1000, 2000 and 4000. `modes()` flags an unresolved mode in 24, 7, 4 and 0 of those, and 154 of 273, 50 of 223, 12 of 212 and 1 of 320 unflagged runs are wrong. All 95 runs that lost the mode report one mode and no warning: a lost mode leaves no label. At `nlive` 1000 the wrong runs are at d = 32 (49: connW 12, sepM 33, sepW 4) and sepM_d18 (8); `modes()` merges the connected modes of connW, so it never flags them. The README says that the absence of a flag is not proof and recommends a second run at twice the `nlive`.
  - *Eggbox.* The clustering holds one cluster through the whole run at `nlive >= 1000` and shares up to 0.1 (8 slots fill only at shares of 0.25 and above or at `nlive` 250, for 7 to 32% of the run), so the hop never acts and the slots are never full (`mode_slots_full` is 0 there); `modes()` now finds the peaks from the gaps between their live points and marks them `tracked: False` (at the sweep's sha, eb4631c, it reported one mode). logZ is unbiased (within 2 se at every `nlive` at share 0.1). The weights of the whole and half peaks are unbiased on average and scatter by 0.94, 0.68, 0.43 and 0.26 in the log at `nlive` 500 to 4000 (exact-draw floor 0.22 to 0.04); each 0.02 peak falls below 10% of its weight (the definition of lost in `bench/validate.py` and in the known limitations) in 32%, 15%, 4% and 0% of the runs, and some peak does in 78%, 35%, 8% and 0%; with a threshold of 0.1% of the weight, some peak is lost in 42%, 7%, 0% and 0% of the runs. The 0.08 peaks are never below 10% from `nlive` 1000.
  - *Memory.* Peak GPU memory of the 40-run batch: 0.3 GiB at d = 10 and 0.7 GiB at d = 32 with `nlive` 1000; 4.0 GiB at d = 64 with `nlive` 4000 (6.8 GiB at share 0.5).
  - *Lazy compaction* (skipping out-of-cube lanes at `num_delete > 1`) was not built: at the default share those lanes are 12 to 16% of the calls on the Gaussians, 19 to 23% on the mixtures and 6 to 8% on the funnel and Rosenbrock, the most it could save.

### Known limitations

- **Small modes.** A mode with fewer than about `5 * ndim` live points is not sampled reliably: its weight is biased or scatters widely, and the mode can be lost. A covariance-adapted walk needs that many points to learn a mode's shape. On one Gaussian with the shape of the 18-D bake-off target's minor mode, 1.7, 3.3 and 6.7 points per dimension left logZ 4.35, 0.21 and 0.03 too high. `modes()` flags such a mode `unresolved` in a run that kept it; raise `nlive`.
- **A mode can be lost with no warning.** A run that loses a mode reports the modes left with no flag (`unresolved` flags a mode only in a run that kept it), and none of the lost runs measured warned. A mode's share of the live points when it separates is its share of the prior volume there, not its mass, so the `nlive` rule of *Defaults* (`5 * ndim / f` to `10 * ndim / f`) is far too low for a narrow heavy mode. On the 18-D target `build_two_mode(18, 0.2)` of `bench.targets` (a 20% mode whose volume is 0.035 of the main mode's) at the default `nlive`, the 20% mode was lost on an H100 at 8851058 in 4 of 32 runs in float32, 4 of 32 in x64 and 1 of 32 in float32 at matmul precision `"highest"`, and on a CPU in x64 in 3 of 24 runs at 8851058 and 2 of 16 on the release code. A lost run reports one mode, with the hop off, and its logZ lies within 2.6 `logzerr` of the truth (14 runs): without the mode logZ is `log(1 - 0.2) = -0.22` lower, less than its `logzerr` of 0.26. On a 4-D mixture of masses 0.6, 0.3 and 0.1 and widths 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 (rotated Gaussians in `[-12, 12]^4`), the 0.6 mode held a median of 8 to 9 of 500 live points when it separated, and it was lost at `nlive=500` in 7 of 32 runs in float32 and 4 of 32 in x64 (at 1000 in 3 and 4 of 64, seeds 0 to 31 and 100 to 131; in 6-D at 1000 in 21 and 22 of 24), with logZ 0.62 to 1.13 low (4.6 to 10.4 `logzerr`; `log(1 - 0.6) = -0.92`). 177 of the 179 runs that kept it flagged it unresolved, as did all 14 runs on the release code (CPU) that kept the 20% mode of the 18-D target, and the warning of `diagnostics()` now says that in other runs such a mode may be lost entirely (`summary()` and `print(result)` show only the flag). If any run flags a mode unresolved, raise `nlive` and compare runs. 1.1 loses such modes less often (M18: 11 → 1 of 128 runs; the 4-D mixture: 97 → 68 of 384 at `nlive=500` and 19 → 6 at 1000; not the 6-D one) and adds `tinyns.compare`; its entry says what remains.
- **Small modes in high dimensions go unreported.** At 32-D and the default `nlive` the clustering did not separate a 6% mode (about 60 live points) in 32 to 36 of 40 `sepW_d32` runs; `modes()` then reports one mode without a warning. The samples carry the mode at its weight (logit scatter 0.14 to 0.17, none lost). float32 and x64 gave the same logZ and mode weights on two- and three-mode mixtures from 10 to 32 dimensions, except below `5 * ndim` live points per mode (`sepM_d18`: logit scatter 0.54 against 0.16).
- **A mode the clustering never split off.** A mode whose live points are gone before the clustering first splits it from its neighbours is merged into the nearest reported mode, with no warning: on a 4-D mixture of masses 0.75, 0.2 and 0.05 along a line, with the 0.05 mode at an end, 26 of 32 runs (nlive 1000) reported two tracked modes; in a triangle, or with the 0.05 mode in the middle, every run reported three. The count is then a lower bound for tracked modes too; the samples, logZ and the summed mass are right.
- **More modes than the clustering separates.** The clustering has 8 slots and splits two ways, so it separates neither more than 8 modes nor modes on a lattice (an eggbox, where it never splits). The evidence is unaffected, but the hop does not balance such modes: on the eggbox at nlive 1000 the masses of the 0.08 and 0.04 modes scatter by 0.38 and 0.61 in logit over seeds, and a 0.02 mode was lost (a weight below 10% of the truth) in 15% of the runs (0.56, 1.5 and 32% at nlive 500). `modes()` marks them `tracked: False` and counts only those that planes across the axes of the unit cube separate; a mode with fewer than 8 live points stays merged with a neighbour.
- **Close modes.** Two modes are reported as one when the likelihood dips by less than 3 between them and the bottom of the dip lies above the posterior median likelihood of at least one of them: equal Gaussians closer than about 2.5 standard deviations in 1-D, 3.3 in 2-D, and up to 5.4 from 7-D on. Farther apart they are two only where the clustering split them: equal Gaussians 5 standard deviations apart in 5-D were one mode, with no warning, in 25 of 32 runs (nlive 500). A close mode of less mass stays in the labels the clustering split the heavier one from. In 2-D, a 0.1 or 0.2 mode was merged at 4.5 to 5.5 standard deviations in 70 of 72 runs, and a 0.1 mode at 6.5 in 18 of 32 (nlive 500), with no warning. logZ and the samples are unaffected.
- **Separated rings in 2-D.** The clustering's split test separates two well-separated rings in 2-D only by chance: its statistic for the true split is at most about 24.5 on average, just under the threshold of 25, and only sampling noise lifts it above. On the Feroz-Hobson 2-D shells (nlive 500) both rings were tracked in 195 of 320 runs, 309 of 320 runs warn, the hop barely balances the rings (the mass of one ring, half the total, scatters by 0.09 to 0.13 in logit with it and 0.14 to 0.15 without, 32 runs each), and 2 of 320 runs reported one mode without a warning. logZ is unaffected.
- **Modes with thin arms or a funnel neck.** When a mode's contours are a core with thin arms or narrow into a neck (a scale parameter pressed against its prior edge and coupled to a location), the clustering cuts each mode into lumps and the hop's ellipsoids cover it poorly; the hop is then often off (its clusters are not eligible) or rarely accepted. On `needle_d18` the hop cut the run-to-run logit scatter of the minor mass from 0.57 to 0.19, against 0.37 to 0.019 for the same modes without arms; on `cauchy_d18` from 0.53 to 0.32; on an 18-D posterior with a funnel neck it gave no gain (0.25 over 3 runs, 0.30 for v0.2.5; hop acceptance 0.6%). logZ is unaffected (`needle_d18`: +0.03 +- 0.08, `cauchy_d18`: -0.09 +- 0.07 from the truth, 16 runs). `diagnostics()` warns, and `run_sd` gives the scatter. On the 18-D posterior `modes()` also gave the minor mode 0.02 to 0.03 less mass than a cut on the physical parameter (the nearest-mode classification gives part of its tail to the wider main mode), and on `cauchy_d18` it merged the two modes into one in 2 of 16 runs, although the clustering had separated them.
- **Very small modes are not reported.** A cluster with at most `ndim` live points at its posterior median is not counted, and each of the three cross-fitted clusterings holds a third of a mode's points: with a 6% mode of 30 live points in 10-D (sepM_d10 at nlive 500), 32 of 64 runs reported one mode and no warning.
- **Curved targets are biased at small `nlive`.** At the default `walks` (60) and `num_delete = nlive // 10`, the logZ of the 10-D Rosenbrock is -0.42 +- 0.11, -0.16 +- 0.05, -0.05 +- 0.04, +0.00 +- 0.02 and +0.00 +- 0.02 from the truth at `nlive` 250, 500, 1000, 2000 and 4000 (-0.09 +- 0.04 in 32 more float32 runs at 1000), with a scatter of 1.64, 0.99, 1.08, 1.00 and 1.08 times the old `logzerr` (40 runs each, defaults sweep). The earlier advice (`walks` of 12 to 25 `* ndim`, a scatter of 2.3 times `logzerr` at the default) was measured before the seed-free covariance and the local walk and no longer holds. `bench/validate.py` still runs its Rosenbrock cases at `walks = 25 * ndim`.
- **Posteriors pressed against the faces of the unit cube.** When a nonlinear prior transform puts the posterior near a corner of the cube in most coordinates (a normal prior with the data several prior sd away), the default `walks` do not decorrelate the chains: logZ comes out low and `logzerr` 3 to 3.7 times too small, though the posterior is right. `ndtri_d6` (the data 3 prior sd off centre in all 6 coordinates) needed 4 times the default `walks`, and twice was not enough; a stronger conflict can need more, so check by doubling `walks`.
- **Expensive likelihoods.** Parallel chains do not reduce the number of likelihood calls, and with `num_delete > 1` the proposals that a `num_delete=1` run skips (outside the cube, or rejected by a hop's or local walk's Hastings test) are evaluated too (`ncall` against `metadata["ncall_valid"]`). In a batch of runs `num_delete=1` evaluates the proposals a single run skips, uncounted (5 to 17% more evaluations than `ncall` while the runs are running), and every run is computed until the last one stops (about `B` times the calls of the longest run). Use `num_delete=1` in a single run when one call already fills the device.
- **Cost of the mode tracking.** About 6% of the run time at 1 ms per likelihood call (H100, 40 batched runs; at most 18% over the validation cells). It is a fixed cost per step, paid by unimodal runs too, so for likelihoods that take microseconds it is a large share: about 40% of a compiled run of the 13-D Gaussian on the H100 (0.93 s against 0.56 s for the first v1 core), and 56%, 46% and 33% of a compiled run of the 8-, 10- and 16-D Gaussians at nlive 1000 on 4 CPU cores (measured by reclustering only at the start, which leaves a Gaussian run bit-identical). The 15 to 20% more per call of a two-mode target (see *Highlights*) compares it with a Gaussian, and both pay this cost. A recluster cadence that adapts would cut it but delay the detection of splits. Compilation takes about 10 s on a GPU.
- **float32.** Likelihood differences below about `1e-7 * |loglike|` are lost; enable x64 for large `|loglike|` or very narrow posteriors. A likelihood built from matrix products resolves about 3 digits under TF32 on an A100 or H100 unless `jax_default_matmul_precision` is "highest" (or x64): on the H100 a 512 x 512 product had a relative error of 2.9e-4 at the default precision against 1.1e-6 at "highest", and the bench likelihoods were off by up to 0.045 nats near the posterior and by 1.6e2 to 2.4e2 nats in the prior bulk.
- **JAX debug flags.** `jax_debug_nans` does not catch a NaN of `loglike` inside the sampler, which maps NaN to `-inf`; `jax_debug_infs` cannot be used, because `-inf` is a legitimate likelihood; and `jax_debug_key_reuse` raises in the first step when the key is a typed key (an int seed or a raw `jax.random.PRNGKey` runs).
- **Insertion test.** With `num_delete > 1` the ranks of one step share their survivors, so the p-values are anti-conservative above `num_delete = nlive // 10`: 5%, 6%, 13% and 30% of correct runs fall below 0.05 at shares of 0.05, 0.1, 0.25 and 0.5 (see Defaults).
- **Finite plateaus with few points above them.** When a plateau holds nearly all the live points, the prior volume above it is estimated from the few that are not, and the estimate is noisy. With 5 to 10 of 500 live points above a plateau (`L = -5` on half the 5-D cube of the toy above), logZ was +0.06 ± 0.04 from the truth at `num_delete=25` (96 runs on two sets of keys) and +0.03 ± 0.07 at `num_delete=1` (24 runs), against +3.18 and +3.14 without the plateau counts; a floor (`max(L, -5)`, 99.1% of the cube on the floor) gave +0.11 ± 0.05 (96 runs) against +4.07. The scatter is large (0.41, 0.35 and 0.52), and `logzerr` counts it (0.35, 0.35 and 0.48 on average; `logz_bootstrap()` 0.35 and 0.44), though the spread is skewed; the old sum gave 0.09 to 0.10, because the plateau deaths gain no information. A likelihood that is `-inf` on all but a small part of the prior (a compact support) is the same case at the start of a run. It carries the same volume noise and, when the support is a few times `1 / nlive` of the prior, a bias below about 0.1 nats, half a `logzerr`: with 1 to 10 of 500 initial points finite in a 10-D box, logZ came out between -0.10 and +0.08 from the truth once that noise is removed (seven sets of 8 to 16 runs, float32 and x64), about as close as with 40 of 500 finite (-0.04 ± 0.04). On an extreme support, 1% of the prior along one axis, about +0.1 remains. When the support is much smaller than `1 / nlive` of the prior and only one initial point falls in it, the run takes the support's volume from that one point: in a 5-D ball of 1.6e-6 of the prior around one initial point, logZ was 10.3 nats high at nlive 100 and 8.5 at 500 (16 runs each, float32) with `logzerr` near 0.8; the volume estimate was 9.1 and 7.5 nats too large, and +1.14 ± 0.06 and +0.95 ± 0.13 remain without it. The insertion test flags those runs. A zero-likelihood region with a fair share of the live points above it is unbiased, with the right `logzerr`, and so is a likelihood that ends on a plateau. Raise `nlive` on such a target.
- **Memory of a heavy likelihood.** A step, and the start of a run, evaluate `num_delete` likelihoods at once, and the default `nlive // 10` does not fit a heavy likelihood: a gravitational-wave population likelihood (259 events, 4096 samples each) asked for 67 GB at `num_delete=200` and a 13-D mock of it 78 GB at 100, on an 80 GB H100. `run` raises `MemoryError` naming what can shrink (`num_delete`, or the number of keys of a batched run); lower it (25 and 100 fitted). There is no automatic cap.
- **Compile cache.** A process keeps up to 8 compiled configurations per kernel; a loop over more configurations than that recompiles.
- **Bit-identical resume** holds on a CPU; on a GPU in float32 a resume after a kill runs in a new process and agrees in distribution only (see the next item).
- **A fixed seed is not bit-reproducible on a GPU in float32.** Five identical runs in separate processes gave four different `logz` (measured on `sepM_d32` on an H100 at afa58dd). On a CPU a fixed seed reproduces to the bit. The results agree in distribution.

## v0.2.5 (2026-10-02)

- **New default on the fast path**: `cluster_swap=True`. Mode weights of separated modes no longer drift with the seed when each mode is large enough. Replacement chains start from live points and cannot cross between separated modes, so each mode's live count did a random walk. On a real 18-D posterior with a 16% minor mode, two seeds gave 7% and 32%. tinyns now clusters the live points on the host between blocks and tracks the clusters across blocks. Once two clusters each have a volume share that predicts at least 2 x `ndim` live points, a tenth of the chain steps become an affine swap `u' = mu_b + L_b L_a^-1 (u - mu_a)` into another cluster's frame. The swap is accepted when `u'` is in the cube, above the threshold and nearest to frame `b`, with the Metropolis factor `det L_b / det L_a`. This is exact for any frames. The frame of the chain's seed is refit without the seed, and the rwalk keeps the global live covariance.
- Measured with tinyns on two-mode Gaussian targets with a 6% minor mode (nlive 500, 20 seeds per cell, swap off -> on). Logit seed scatter of the minor-mode mass: 0.46 -> 0.15 and 0.36 -> 0.10 at 4-D (two minor-mode shapes), 0.83 -> 0.16 at 10-D, 1.28 -> 0.39 at 18-D (200 seeds; the prototype gives 0.36 there). Mean mass with the swap on: 0.060, 0.060, 0.057 +/- 0.002 and 0.058 +/- 0.001. An eligibility threshold of 3 x `ndim` switched the swap off too early at 18-D (0.52, 40 seeds, one seed lost the mode). logZ bias and scatter are unchanged, and likelihood calls fell by 2% to 5%. A minor mode with fewer live points than about 2 x `ndim` (20 at 10-D, 36 at 18-D) gets no reliable benefit: the swap stays off or flickers, and the weights drift as before.
- On a real 18-D posterior (darksirens rung 5, BPL+2G, low-H0 mode holding 18-22% of the mass by a split-prior reference) at nlive 1000, 8 seeds per arm: the swap gives a low-H0 mass of 0.188 on average (logit sd 0.30) against 0.135 (logit sd 0.44) without it, and logZ -678.45 +/- 0.23 against the reference -678.3 +/- 0.2. At nlive 500 both arms come out near 0.08: the mode holds only about 28 live points (1.5 x `ndim`) when it is first detected, too few for the swap.
- A unimodal run that the clustering does not split is bit-identical to v0.2.4 for the same seed; a test pins fixed-seed fingerprints. On synthetic single ellipsoids, uniform boxes and the 13-D Gaussian there were no false splits. Real posteriors do get split sometimes: 3 of 10 seeds on a 13-D mock spectral-siren fit and 2 of 2 on another, none on a 16-D dark-siren fit. The move is exact either way, and those runs agree with v0.2.4 and the reference within `logzerr` (13-D means -58.578 vs -58.581, reference -58.623 +/- 0.029). The clustering runs on the host and never touches the PRNG stream. It costs about 12 ms per update at 13-D (one update every 128 iterations): 2.0 s of a 29 s run on a 13-D Gaussian.
- `result.metadata` reports `cluster_count_history` (`[iteration, clusters]` at each change), `cluster_swap_proposals`, `cluster_swap_accepts`, `cluster_min_population` and `cluster_modes`. `cluster_modes` gives, for each mode, its mass, its smallest live count between detection and its posterior median, and `urn_logit_sd`, the seed scatter of `logit(mass)` the drift would cause without the swap. Also `cluster_host_s`.
- `cluster_swap` applies to `sample="rwalk"`, `kernel="jax"`, `bound="none"`, `rwalk_proposal="live-cov"`, `replacement_chains=1` and `jax_block_size > 1`. Elsewhere it defaults to False, and an explicit `cluster_swap=True` raises `NotImplementedError`. Checkpoints record it and the cluster tracker's state. A checkpoint without the key reads as `cluster_swap=False`, so resume a v0.2.4 checkpoint with `cluster_swap=False`.

## v0.2.4 (2026-10-02)

- **Changed default**: `min_accepts=0` (was 1). A replacement chain that never moved is now kept as a copy of its seed, instead of being discarded and retried. The retry rule was biased: chains fail to move more often where the proposal fits poorly, so retrying under-sampled those regions. Measured with tinyns itself on two-mode Gaussian targets with a true 6% minor mode (nlive 500): at 4-D the old rule gave 2.9% +/- 0.3% and a logZ bias of +0.13 +/- 0.03, the new default 6.3% +/- 0.4% and +0.02 +/- 0.03 (40 seeds each); at 10-D, 1.7% +/- 0.6% against 6.7% +/- 1.7% (18 seeds each). Call counts are unchanged. A kept chain must still end inside the constraint, and an unmoved chain only counts if its seed is strictly above the threshold. Pass `min_accepts=1` for the old behaviour; checkpoints written with the old default need `min_accepts=1` to resume.

## v0.2.3 (2026-10-01)

- A loop over mock datasets no longer recompiles for every dataset. The fast-path kernel caches (block kernel, rwalk kernel and the initial live-point pass) were keyed on the identity of `loglike` and `prior_transform`, so each `jax.tree_util.Partial(loglike_fn, data_i)` built and compiled a new block kernel. They are now keyed on the callable's structure: a pytree callable's treedef and static leaves, with its array leaves passed as jit arguments, so JAX's jit cache keys the compiled program on their shapes and dtypes. On CPU, four `Partial` datasets of the same shape (1-D, 10k points, fixed `maxiter`) took 2 backend compiles for every new dataset in v0.2.2 (block kernel and live-point pass) and none in v0.2.3. Each result is bit-identical to a fresh run on that dataset. The live-point pass is now cached too; v0.2.2 compiled it again on every run, even for the same callable.
- Finished runs no longer keep their datasets alive. The `lru_cache(maxsize=32)` held up to 32 callables and so their data: in a four-dataset probe, v0.2.2 retained all four. The caches now hold no array leaves. Closures and plain functions are cached by identity through weak references, and their hoisted constants are dropped when the closure is garbage collected, so a dataset is freed once you drop it. Each closure object still compiles its own kernels; use a pytree callable to share one compile across datasets.
- Plain functions and small closures give bit-identical results to v0.2.2: a test pins fixed-seed `logz`, `samples`, `samples_u`, `logl` and `ncall` for the block and per-iteration paths. `Partial` and hoisted-closure runs were also checked to be bit-identical.
- `result.metadata` reports wall-time telemetry: `wall_time_s` (sampling wall time of the call), `compile_s` (time to the end of the first block, which carries the compiles) and `mean_ms_per_call` (wall time per likelihood call after the first block). Progress lines show `calls/s` since the first block, and callback states carry `calls_per_s`. There are no new arguments.
- The README notes that separate processes in a campaign can share compiles through JAX's persistent cache (`jax.config.update("jax_compilation_cache_dir", path)`). On CPU, a second process with a different dataset read its compiled programs from the cache and reached the end of its first block in 0.72 s instead of 1.62 s.

## v0.2.2 (2026-10-01)

- With the default single chain (`replacement_chains=1`), a live-cov proposal that leaves the unit cube no longer calls the likelihood. It is rejected either way, so it now takes a `lax.cond` branch that returns `-inf` without evaluating. `ncall` counts likelihood evaluations actually made; the acceptance rate and the batch bookkeeping use the proposal count. Samples and logZ are identical to v0.2.1; `logl` differs only at the 1e-13 rounding level, from XLA fusing the call inside the branch. Measured on an H100 against v0.2.1: a 13-D mock spectral-siren fit made 55% fewer calls (521k to 233k) and ran 1.8x faster (226 s to 124 s); a 1-D dark-siren fit made 12% fewer calls and ran 1.12x faster. With more than one chain, a `vmap`ped `cond` would evaluate both branches, so nothing is skipped.

## v0.2.1 (2026-09-30)

- On the fast path, closures now get the pytree-callable treatment automatically. A `loglike` or `prior_transform` without array leaves is traced once (`jax.make_jaxpr`), and every jaxpr constant with at least 4096 elements (a captured `jax.Array` or `np.ndarray`, including operands of a nested `jax.jit` call) is passed to the compiled kernels as a jit argument; smaller constants stay embedded. On a 1-D GW likelihood with 2.5M-row arrays (H100), the closure form took two backend compiles of 74 s and 78 s (first block after 172 s), and the pytree form 5.3 s and 6.8 s (first block after 23 s). With hoisting, the same closure reaches its first block after 21.6 s, the same as the pytree form (21.3 s). Through the darksirens ladder harness (a plain closure), a 1-D dark-siren run on the mock R1 fixture dropped from 861 s and 12.0 GB peak RSS to 387 s and 2.2 GB, against dynesty's 679 s. A 4-D real-data run dropped from 4.8 GB to 1.5 GB peak RSS. There is no API change. Closures with only small constants and plain functions are not traced into a new form and give bit-identical results to v0.2.0. A hoisted closure gives the same results as the equivalent `jax.tree_util.Partial`. If tracing fails, the callable keeps closure semantics and one debug-level log message is emitted per callable. Arrays captured inside the body of an inner `jax.jit` function stay embedded. Pytree callables remain the explicit form.
- `walks` now defaults to 12 in 1-D. In a 20-seed sweep, 1-D was unbiased from 10 walks (bias 0.2 x `logzerr`); from 2-D on, the default stays `max(25, 6 * ndim)`.

## v0.2.0 (2026-09-30)

- Added `rwalk_proposal="live-cov"`: each rwalk step is `step_scale * L @ z`, with `L` the Cholesky factor of the live-point covariance in the unit cube. Moves that leave the cube are rejected rather than reflected, and the step scale always adapts toward `rwalk_target_accept`. It is supported for unbounded `sample="rwalk"`, `kernel="jax"` with a fixed `replacement_chains`; other combinations raise `NotImplementedError`.
- **Changed defaults**: `NestedSampler(loglike, prior_transform, ndim).run(key)` and `run_static_nested` now run the fast path: `sample="rwalk"`, `kernel="jax"`, `rwalk_proposal="live-cov"`, `jax_block_size=32`, `walks=max(25, 6 * ndim)` and an initial `step_scale=0.5`. The old defaults were `sample="prior"`, `kernel="python"`, `rwalk_proposal="isotropic"`, `jax_block_size=1`, `walks=25` and `step_scale=0.1`; pass them explicitly to keep the old behavior. The default `kernel="jax"` needs JAX-traceable `loglike` and `prior_transform`.
- Where live-cov is unsupported (`kernel="python"`, a bound, or a `replacement_chain_schedule`), an unset `rwalk_proposal` falls back to `"isotropic"` with `step_scale=0.1` and `jax_block_size=1`, and `sample="prior"` defaults to `kernel="python"`. An explicit `rwalk_proposal="live-cov"` in those combinations still raises.
- Why: the fixed isotropic unit-cube step (`step_scale=0.1`) did not follow the contracting live set, so acceptance collapsed and the cost per iteration grew geometrically at d >= 4. On anisotropic correlated Gaussians, d = 2..18, live-cov keeps the likelihood calls per iteration flat at `walks`. Unbiased evidence needs `walks` of about 5-6 x `ndim`: `walks=25` biased logZ high by +0.5 nats at 13D and +1.4 nats at 18D, while `walks >= 5 * ndim` was within about 0.2 nats, with seed scatter matching the reported `logzerr`.
- With `kernel="jax"`, the initial live points are now evaluated in one compiled pass.
- `loglike` and `prior_transform` may be JAX pytree callables (`jax.tree_util.Partial(loglike_fn, data)`, an equinox-style Module, a registered dataclass with `__call__`). On the fast path (unbounded JAX rwalk: block kernel, per-iteration kernel, rescue draws and the initial live-point pass) their array leaves are passed to the compiled kernels as jit arguments instead of being embedded as constants. On a GW likelihood with 2.5M-row selection arrays (H100) this cut compile time from 76 s to 15 s and peak host RSS from 10.7 GB to 2.8 GB. Detection is automatic; plain functions and closures give bit-identical results to before. Bounded, fused-bounded and `replacement_chain_schedule` kernels keep closure semantics.
- Checkpoints record the resolved `walks`, `step_scale`, `rwalk_proposal` and `jax_block_size`, so a resume with default arguments matches a fresh run with default arguments. Checkpoints without `rwalk_proposal` are read as `"isotropic"` (no format-version bump).
- The benchmark and validation harnesses (`bench_static.py`, `overnight_jax_validation.py`, `validation/run_validation.py`) pin `rwalk_proposal="isotropic"` and `jax_block_size=1` unless a block size is requested, so their configurations are unchanged.

## v0.1.0-alpha

- Narrowed TinyNS public sampler surface to `sample="rwalk"` and `sample="prior"`.
- Removed legacy `slice`, `rslice`, and `sample="bound"` public sampler paths.
- Documented the recommended unbounded isotropic JAX rwalk fast path with `jax_block_size=32`.
- Added cached JAX block rwalk validation summaries and release checklist commands.
- Added optional B16/B32/B64/B128 block-size smoke documentation; B32 remains the default recommendation.
- Added a self-contained 10D GW-like stress-test template with diagnostic figures.
- Added or documented robust block-mode termination behavior for late replacement failures.
- Added a `logzerr` diagnostics breakdown and additional rwalk/replacement telemetry (`repl_ncall`, `repl_chains`, rescue/usage stage counts) to results and metadata.
- Added `NestedSamplingResult.logz_bootstrap(...)` (exported `LogZBootstrap`), a simulated-weights evidence-uncertainty estimator: it jitters the prior-volume shrinkages from their `Beta(nlive, 1)` law over the stored dead-point sequence and recomputes `logz` many times, returning the realization mean, standard deviation, and 16/50/84 percentiles. This captures the skewed prior-volume path uncertainty that the analytic `sqrt(H/nlive)` cannot. It is pure post-processing (no sampler changes) and does not capture sampling bias, so it is a lower bound on true single-run uncertainty when the constrained sampler is under-decorrelated (e.g. an inflated `insertion_rank_mean_z`).
- Added an experimental `rwalk_adaptive_step_scale` JAX-only rwalk option that adapts the isotropic proposal scale from constrained-replacement acceptance telemetry (default off, `step_scale=0.1` unchanged); the acceptance telemetry it adapts from was fixed to reflect true rwalk move acceptance rather than an earlier proxy.
- **Removed** `rwalk_proposal="live-cov"` and `rwalk_cov_jitter`: validation found concerning evidence pulls, and per-axis unit-cube reflection is not symmetry-preserving for correlated proposals. `rwalk_proposal` stays and now accepts only `"isotropic"`; passing anything else raises a clear removal error.
- **Removed** the `replacement_chain_schedule` + `jax_block_size > 1` combination: the in-JIT adaptive kernel evaluated all `max_chains` likelihoods at every stage regardless of schedule position, saving no compute while understating `ncall`/`chains_used` telemetry. Adaptive replacement-chain schedules remain supported at `jax_block_size=1` (the default).
- Fixed block-mode `ncall` undercounting failed-offset and rescue-ladder likelihood calls on an unrescued replacement failure; these are now always counted, matching per-iteration accounting.
- Fixed checkpoint cadence so `checkpoint_interval` is honored in block mode: checkpoints are now written on elapsed nested-iteration deltas rather than an exact-modulo check, so a large `jax_block_size` no longer skips the first several periodic saves (e.g. block 32 / interval 100 previously did not checkpoint before iteration 800).
- Fixed resume labeling: a resumed run that had already converged now reports `success=True, "converged"` instead of an incorrect `"maxiter reached"` message, and a resumed run at or past `maxiter` without convergence is labeled `"maxiter reached"` rather than defaulting to `"converged"`; fixed in both block and per-iteration modes.
- Persisted `effective_step_scale` in checkpoints as an optional `.npz` field (no checkpoint format-version bump; older checkpoints without the field still load) so adaptive step-scale state survives resume; added `rwalk_adaptive_step_scale`/`rwalk_target_accept` config validation on resume.
- Added a warning (not an error) when `NestedSampler` receives unknown keyword arguments; unknown kwargs are still stored, preserving dynesty drop-in compatibility.
- Validated `maxiter >= 1`; a fresh run with `maxiter=0` previously fell through and was mislabeled `"converged"`.
- Deduplicated run-loop state/bound-rebuild helpers, bound-rwalk JAX wrappers, and tuple-based ellipsoid-bound primitives; behavior-preserving, no public API change.
- Kept bounds, fused bounds, and larger block sizes experimental.

### Release caveats

TinyNS v0.1.0-alpha is intended as a small static nested sampler with a validated low-dimensional JAX rwalk fast path. High-dimensional, strongly curved, or multimodal targets require target-specific validation. The included 10D GW-like benchmark is a stress test for constrained-replacement mixing, not a production GW parameter-estimation pipeline.
